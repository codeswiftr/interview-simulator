# Amazon Interview Guide: Mastering the Leadership Principles

*Amazon hires for their 16 Leadership Principles. Miss one, and you are out. Here is how to prepare for the most principle-obsessed interview process in tech.*

---

Amazon's interview process is unique because it is built entirely around their **16 Leadership Principles**. Every question maps to a principle. Every answer is scored against a principle. Understanding this is the difference between an offer and a rejection.

## Amazon's Interview Process Overview

| Stage | Duration | Format |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Online Assessment | 90 min | Coding + Work Simulation |
| Phone Screen | 60 min | Coding + LP (Leadership Principles) |
| Onsite (Loop) | 5-6 rounds | 45-60 min each |
| Bar Raiser Review | 1-2 weeks | Final decision authority |
| Offer Stage | Variable | Negotiation begins |

**The Loop Breakdown:**
- **2-3 Coding/System Design rounds** — Technical competence
- **3-4 Behavioral rounds** — Leadership Principles deep-dive
- **1 Bar Raiser round** — Special interviewer with veto power

**Critical:** The Bar Raiser is not there to be your friend. They are there to ensure you raise the bar compared to current Amazonians at that level.

**Timeline:** 3-6 weeks from application to offer.

## The 5 Most Important Leadership Principles (and Their Questions)

While Amazon has 16 principles, these 5 appear in virtually every interview loop:

### 1. Customer Obsession

**Definition:** "Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They work vigorously to earn and keep customer trust."

**Common Questions:**
- "Tell me about a time you obsessed over a customer's needs."
- "Describe a time you went above and beyond for a customer."
- "Tell me about a time you received negative customer feedback and how you handled it."

**What Amazon wants:** Evidence that you *start* with customer needs, not technology. They want to see you making trade-offs that favor the customer even when it is harder.

**STAR Structure Tip:**
- **Situation:** Customer-facing problem with measurable impact
- **Task:** Your goal was to solve it
- **Action:** How you deeply understood the customer need, not just the feature request
- **Result:** Customer outcome first, business outcome second

**Key phrase to use:** "I worked backwards from the customer need..."

---

### 2. Ownership

**Definition:** "Leaders are owners. They think long term and don't sacrifice long-term value for short-term results."

**Common Questions:**
- "Tell me about a time you took on something outside your job responsibilities."
- "Describe a time you fixed a problem that wasn't yours to fix."
- "Tell me about a time you had to make a decision without your manager's approval."

**What Amazon wants:** Evidence that you act like an owner, not an employee. You identify gaps and fill them without being asked.

**STAR Structure Tip:**
- **Situation:** A problem others were ignoring or deferring
- **Task:** No one explicitly asked you to solve it
- **Action:** How you identified the issue, took responsibility, and drove resolution
- **Result:** Long-term value created, not just short-term fix

**Key phrase to use:** "I noticed no one was owning this, so I..."

---

### 3. Dive Deep

**Definition:** "Leaders operate at all levels, stay connected to the details, audit frequently, and are skeptical when metrics differ from reality."

**Common Questions:**
- "Tell me about a time you used data to uncover a problem."
- "Describe a time you went deep into the details to solve an issue."
- "Tell me about a time metrics looked good but something was wrong."

**What Amazon wants:** Evidence that you do not accept surface-level answers. You dig into logs, data, and code until you find the root cause.

**STAR Structure Tip:**
- **Situation:** A problem with unclear root cause
- **Task:** Identify what is actually happening
- **Action:** Step-by-step investigation — what data you pulled, what logs you read, what experiments you ran
- **Result:** Root cause identified and fixed

**Key phrase to use:** "I dug into the [logs/metrics/data] and discovered..."

---

### 4. Bias for Action

**Definition:** "Speed matters in business. Many decisions are reversible and do not need extensive study."

**Common Questions:**
- "Tell me about a time you made a decision with incomplete information."
- "Describe a time you had to move fast and take a risk."
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with the approach of doing more analysis."

**What Amazon wants:** Evidence that you know when to act vs. when to analyze. You calculate the cost of delay and make reversible decisions quickly.

**STAR Structure Tip:**
- **Situation:** Time-sensitive decision with incomplete data
- **Task:** Make progress despite uncertainty
- **Action:** How you assessed reversibility, consulted stakeholders quickly, and decided
- **Result:** Speed delivered value; course-corrected if needed

**Key phrase to use:** "Given this was a two-way door decision, I..."

---

### 5. Deliver Results

**Definition:** "Leaders focus on the key inputs and deliver them with the right quality and in a timely fashion."

**Common Questions:**
- "Tell me about your proudest professional accomplishment."
- "Describe a time you exceeded expectations."
- "Tell me about a time you had to deliver despite significant obstacles."

**What Amazon wants:** Evidence that you get things done. Not just effort — actual outcomes that mattered to the business.

**STAR Structure Tip:**
- **Situation:** Ambitious goal with real obstacles
- **Task:** Deliver a specific, measurable outcome
- **Action:** How you identified key inputs, managed the work, and overcame blockers
- **Result:** Quantified business impact — revenue, cost savings, efficiency gains

**Key phrase to use:** "I delivered [specific metric] by focusing on..."

## How to Structure Amazon Answers (STAR + Principles)

Amazon uses a modified STAR method. They want more detail in the Action section and explicit naming of the principle you demonstrated.

| Element | Standard STAR | Amazon-Weighted |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| Situation | 10% | 10% |
| Task | 10% | 10% |
| Action | 60% | **70%** |
| Result | 20% | **10%** |

**The Amazon Difference:** They want granular detail on what *you* did, not the team. Use "I" statements exclusively.

### The Amazon Answer Formula:

1. **State the principle:** "This demonstrates Customer Obsession..."
2. **Situation (30 seconds):** Set the stage
3. **Task (30 seconds):** Define your goal
4. **Action (2-3 minutes):** Detailed, step-by-step what YOU did
5. **Result (30 seconds):** Quantified outcome
6. **What would you do differently?** (Amazon loves this)

## The "Bar Raiser" Round: What You Need to Know

The Bar Raiser is a specially trained interviewer who:
- Represents the long-term interests of Amazon
- Can veto any hire, regardless of other feedback
- Focuses heavily on Leadership Principles
- Asks the toughest follow-up questions

### Bar Raiser Red Flags:
- Vague answers without specific details
- Focusing on "we" instead of "I"
- No clear metrics or outcomes
- Inability to articulate what you learned
- Making excuses or blaming others

### Bar Raiser Green Flags:
- Detailed, almost obsessive specificity
- Owning failures and learnings
- Connecting every answer to a Leadership Principle
- Demonstrating long-term thinking
- Showing customer obsession in unexpected places

## Additional Critical Principles

While the top 5 appear most frequently, be prepared with stories for:

| Principle | What They Look For |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Earn Trust** | Admitting mistakes, giving credit, being transparent |
| **Hire and Develop the Best** | Mentoring, raising team standards, making tough hiring calls |
| **Insist on Highest Standards** | Rejecting mediocre work, pushing for excellence |
| **Think Big** | Ambitious vision, transformative ideas, not incrementalism |
| **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** | Respectful disagreement, data-driven arguments, committing once decided |

## Final Tips for Amazon Interviews

**Before the Interview:**
- Prepare 2 stories per Leadership Principle (32 total)
- Study Amazon's PR/FAQ format — "working backwards"
- Know Amazon's businesses deeply (AWS, Prime, Marketplace, etc.)

**During the Interview:**
- State the principle you are demonstrating upfront
- Use "I" exclusively — never "we" for things you did
- Be specific to the point of being granular
- Expect aggressive follow-up: "Why that decision?" "What else did you consider?"
- Have a "what would you do differently" ready for every story

**After the Interview:**
- Send thank-you emails referencing specific discussions
- Be patient — the Bar Raiser review takes time
- Prepare for possible "loop" interviews if feedback is mixed
