## Resource 2: Pre-Interview Anxiety Checklist

**Format:** PDF (2 pages)
**Target Persona:** The Anxious Job Seeker (Alex), The Imposter (Sam)
**Pillar:** Interview Anxiety & Confidence
**Email Gate:** Yes

### Content Outline

**Page 1: The Night Before**

□ **Logistics locked**
- Interview link/address confirmed
- Interviewer names noted
- Company research complete (recent news, product updates)

□ **Materials ready**
- Resume printed (if in-person)
- Portfolio/examples accessible
- Questions for interviewer prepared

□ **Environment set**
- Quiet space secured
- Camera/audio tested (if virtual)
- Backup plan for tech issues

□ **Body prep**
- Clothes laid out
- Alarm set (1 hour buffer)
- Light dinner, limited alcohol
- Sleep target: 7+ hours

**Page 2: Morning of Interview**

□ **90 minutes before**
- Light exercise (10-min walk)
- Shower + professional clothes
- Review your 5 key stories (don't cram new material)

□ **30 minutes before**
- Eat something light
- Hydrate (water, not excessive coffee)
- Use the bathroom

□ **10 minutes before**
- Physiological sigh: Double inhale, long exhale (3x)
- Power pose: 2 minutes standing tall
- Smile (triggers positive emotional state)
- Affirmation: "I'm here to share what I know"

□ **Final check**
- Phone on silent
- Notifications disabled
- Camera at eye level
- Light source in front of you

**Bottom of Page 2:**
Remember: The interviewer wants you to succeed. A good hire makes their job easier.

## Resource 6: Remote Interview Setup Guide

**Format:** PDF (3 pages)
**Target Persona:** All personas
**Pillar:** Efficient Prep & Remote Interviews
**Email Gate:** Yes

### Content Outline

**Page 1: Video Setup**

**Camera**
- Eye level (stack books under laptop if needed)
- Arm's length from face
- Looking at camera lens, not screen (practice this)

**Lighting**
- Primary light source IN FRONT of you
- No windows behind you (backlit = dark face)
- Avoid overhead lighting only (creates shadows)

**Background**
- Neutral, uncluttered
- Blur if necessary
- No distracting movement

**Page 2: Audio Setup**

**Microphone**
- Wired headphones > wireless (no latency)
- Any headphones > laptop mic (reduces echo)
- Test audio levels before interview

**Environment**
- Quiet room, door closed
- Inform housemates of interview time
- Have backup location if needed

**Page 3: Tech Checklist**

□ Test video platform 24 hours before
□ Update browser/app
□ Close unnecessary applications
□ Restart computer morning of
□ Have phone ready as backup device
□ Know IT support contact for platform
□ Have interviewer's phone number (for emergencies)

**Energy Adjustment**
What feels normal in person reads as flat on video.
- Speak 10-15% louder than normal
- Use more facial expressions
- Lean slightly forward to show engagement

### Content Outline

**Page 1: Week 1**

**Day 1-2: Story Bank (2h/day)**
□ Write 5 behavioral stories using CAR+ framework
□ Practice each story out loud
□ Time yourself (target: 90-120 seconds each)
□ Record and review at least 2 stories

**Day 3-4: Technical Refresh (2h/day)**
□ Review 5 core coding patterns for your target role
□ Do 3-5 practice problems (not 50)
□ Focus on explaining your thought process

**Day 5: Company Research (1h)**
□ Read recent company news/blog posts
□ Understand the product and business model
□ Prepare 3-5 questions for interviewers

**Weekend: System Design Basics (4h)**
□ Review fundamental building blocks
□ Practice 1-2 common designs
□ Focus on asking clarifying questions

**Page 2: Week 2**

**Day 8-9: Mock Interviews (2h/day)**
□ Do at least 2 mock behavioral interviews
□ Get feedback on communication, not just content
□ Record if possible

**Day 10: Gap Analysis (2h)**
□ Review mock feedback
□ Identify top 2-3 areas to improve
□ Targeted practice on weak areas

**Day 11-12: Polish (1.5h/day)**
□ Refine stories based on feedback
□ Practice opening ("Tell me about yourself")
□ Review your questions for interviewers

**Weekend: Final Prep**
□ One final mock interview
□ Confirm logistics (time, platform, interviewer names)
□ Light review—no new material

**Page 3: Interview Day**

**Morning**
□ Light exercise (walk, stretch)
□ Healthy breakfast
□ Review stories one time (don't cram)

**30 Minutes Before**
□ Tech check (camera, audio, internet)
□ Water nearby
□ Physiological sigh breathing (3x)

**After Interview**
□ Send thank-you email within 24 hours
□ Note what went well/poorly (for next time)

## Resource 8: Salary Negotiation Scripts

**Format:** PDF (4 pages)
**Target Persona:** All personas
**Pillar:** Efficient Prep & Remote Interviews
**Email Gate:** Yes

### Content Outline

**Page 1: The Fundamentals**

**Rule 1:** Never accept on the spot
"Thank you so much for this offer. I'm really excited about the opportunity. Can I have 48 hours to review everything?"

**Rule 2:** Always negotiate (they expect it)
Average increase from negotiating: 10-15%
On $150K base, that's $15-22K per year

**Rule 3:** Negotiate by phone, not email
Harder to say no to a person than to a screen

**Page 2: The Core Script**

**Opening:**
"I'm really excited about this opportunity. Based on my experience with [specific skill] and my research on market rates, I was hoping we could get to [$X]. Is there flexibility there?"

**Why it works:**
- Shows enthusiasm (they need to feel you'll accept)
- Provides justification (not just "I want more")
- Leaves room for discussion (not an ultimatum)

**Page 3: Handling Objections**

**"That's above our budget"**
"I understand. Are there other components of the package that have flexibility? I'm thinking about signing bonus, equity, or additional PTO."

**"We don't negotiate on base salary"**
"I appreciate you sharing that. What about a performance review at 6 months with potential salary adjustment?"

**"This is our best offer"**
"I appreciate that. Can I ask what the promotion timeline looks like, and what the compensation increase would be?"

**Page 4: What NOT to Say**

✗ "Can you do better?" (too vague)
✗ "I need..." (sounds desperate)
✗ "Another company offered..." (only if true)
✗ "I'll have to decline" (ultimatums backfire)

**Research Resources**
- Levels.fyi (tech-specific)
- Glassdoor (general)
- Blind (anonymous reports)
- Your network (most accurate)

## Resource 9: Interview Day Confidence Routine

**Format:** PDF (2 pages)
**Target Persona:** The Imposter (Sam), The Anxious Job Seeker (Alex)
**Pillar:** Interview Anxiety & Confidence
**Email Gate:** Yes

### Content Outline

**Page 1: Morning Routine (90 min before)**

**Physical (30 min)**
□ 10-minute walk or light exercise
□ Shower (temperature shift resets nervous system)
□ Dress professionally (even for remote—affects mindset)

**Mental (15 min)**
□ Review your 5 key stories (don't read, recite)
□ Visualize yourself answering confidently
□ Remind yourself: "I'm here to share what I know"

**Fuel (15 min)**
□ Light meal (protein + complex carbs)
□ Coffee/tea if that's your routine (don't change habits)
□ Water—stay hydrated

**Page 2: Pre-Interview (30 min before)**

**Environment (10 min)**
□ Tech check (camera, audio, internet)
□ Clean space (clutter affects mental state)
□ Water within reach
□ Phone on silent, notifications off

**Nervous System Reset (5 min)**
□ Physiological sigh: Double inhale, long exhale (3x)
□ Box breathing: 4 sec in, 4 sec hold, 4 sec out, 4 sec hold (3x)
□ Smile and hold for 30 seconds (triggers positive emotions)

**Mindset (5 min)**
□ "They want me to succeed"
□ "I belong in this room"
□ "I'm prepared and I'm ready"
□ Write down one thing you're proud of professionally

**Final 10 min**
□ Stand in power pose (2 min)
□ Review interviewer names
□ Take 3 deep breaths
□ Join call

## Resource 10: Senior Behavioral Question Bank

**Format:** PDF (8 pages)
**Target Persona:** The Senior Aiming Higher (Morgan)
**Pillar:** Senior & System Design Interviews
**Email Gate:** Yes

### Content Outline

**Page 1: How Senior Interviews Differ**

At mid-level, they ask: "Can you do the work?"
At senior level, they ask: "Can you lead the work?"

Senior behavioral questions probe:
- Strategic thinking (not just execution)
- Influence without authority
- Handling ambiguity
- Developing others
- Making trade-offs

**Pages 2-3: Strategy & Vision (10 questions)**

1. Tell me about a time you identified a problem no one else saw.
2. Describe a situation where you had to make a significant technical decision with incomplete information.
3. Tell me about a time you changed direction on a project based on new information.
4. Describe a technical investment that didn't pan out. How did you handle it?
5. Tell me about a time you had to balance short-term delivery with long-term technical health.
6. Describe a situation where you had to say no to a stakeholder.
7. Tell me about a time you killed a project or feature.
8. Describe how you've influenced your team's or org's technical direction.
9. Tell me about a time you identified and addressed technical debt proactively.
10. Describe a situation where you had to make a decision that was unpopular.

[Example answer for #5]

**Pages 4-5: Leadership & Influence (10 questions)**

1. Tell me about a time you led a project with engineers more senior than you.
2. Describe a situation where you had to align multiple teams with different priorities.
3. Tell me about a time you helped an underperforming team member.
4. Describe a time you had to deliver difficult feedback to a peer.
5. Tell me about a situation where you built consensus on a contentious technical decision.
6. Describe a time you mentored someone through a significant challenge.
7. Tell me about a time you had to lead through a crisis.
8. Describe a situation where you delegated something important and it didn't go well.
9. Tell me about a time you had to manage up—influencing your manager or leadership.
10. Describe a time you built a team or significantly improved a team's culture.

[Example answer for #4]

**Pages 6-7: Execution & Delivery (10 questions)**

1. Tell me about a project that was significantly more complex than initially estimated.
2. Describe a time you had to deliver with significant constraints (time, resources, technical).
3. Tell me about a situation where you inherited a codebase or system in poor shape.
4. Describe a time you had to manage scope creep on a project.
5. Tell me about a launch that didn't go as planned.
6. Describe a situation where you had to coordinate across multiple teams and time zones.
7. Tell me about a time you had to make quality trade-offs to meet a deadline.
8. Describe a time you significantly improved a process or system.
9. Tell me about a situation where you had to recover from a production incident.
10. Describe a time you had to context-switch between multiple high-priority projects.

[Example answer for #9]

**Page 8: The Meta-Principle**

At senior levels, every answer should demonstrate:
- **Judgment:** Why you made the choices you made
- **Scope:** Impact beyond your immediate work
- **Growth:** What you learned and how you've changed
- **Others:** How you enabled or developed people around you

Questions to ask yourself for each story:
- Did I explain the trade-offs I considered?
- Did I show impact beyond my immediate team?
- Did I acknowledge what I'd do differently?
- Did I mention how I helped others succeed?
