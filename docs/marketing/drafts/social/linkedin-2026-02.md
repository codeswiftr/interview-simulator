## Post 1: The 60-Second Anxiety Hack
**Target:** Alex (The Anxious Job Seeker)
**Hook:** You walk into the interview room, and everything you know evaporates.

**Body:**
Your palms sweat. Your voice shakes. That answer you rehearsed fifty times? Gone.

This isn't a "lack of preparation" problem. It's a biology problem.

When you're in panic mode, your prefrontal cortex—the part of your brain responsible for complex thinking—goes partially offline. Your body has decided you're in danger and is prioritizing survival over logical thought.

Telling yourself to "just calm down" doesn't work. You need to convince your nervous system that there's no fire.

Try the **Physiological Sigh**:
1. Take a deep breath in through your nose.
2. At the very top, sneak in a second, shorter inhale.
3. Slow, long exhale through your mouth.

One cycle takes 10 seconds. Three cycles recalibrate your nervous system.

Stop trying to think your way out of anxiety. Breathe your way out of it.

**CTA:**
Have you tried physiological hacks before an interview? What's your "go-to" for staying calm?

## Post 2: Why STAR is Making Your Interviews Boring
**Target:** All Personas (Behavioral Mastery)
**Hook:** Unpopular opinion: The STAR method is making your interviews worse.

**Body:**
Every prep resource teaches it. Every candidate uses it. And most behavioral answers sound exactly the same.

The issue? STAR tells you to start with "Situation"—usually 2-3 sentences of boring context.

But your interviewer doesn't care about the background. They care about what YOU did.

Try the **Conflict-First** approach instead:

Start in the middle, where things got hard.
→ "Everyone said the migration was impossible."
→ "Two days before launch, we found a critical bug."
→ "The customer was about to churn."

THEN give minimal context.
THEN explain what you did.

The difference:
STAR: "The situation was our team was behind schedule..."
Conflict-first: "We had 48 hours and needed 2 weeks of work."

One sounds like a report. One sounds like a story worth hearing.

**CTA:**
Review your top 3 interview stories today. Can you move the "conflict" to the first sentence?

## Post 3: The Senior Interview Trap
**Target:** Morgan (The Senior Aiming Higher)
**Hook:** The biggest mistake senior engineers make in system design interviews: Designing immediately.

**Body:**
"Design a URL shortener."
*Starts drawing boxes*

This works at L4. It fails at Staff.

At senior levels, they aren't testing if you CAN design. They're testing if you know WHICH design fits THESE constraints.

The fix: Spend the first 5 minutes ONLY asking questions.

→ "What's the scale? (1k users or 1B users?)"
→ "What are we optimizing for? (Latency? Cost? Availability?)"
→ "What constraints exist? (Regulatory? Technical debt?)"

This isn't stalling. It's demonstrating exactly what senior engineers get paid to do: Understanding the problem before jumping to solutions.

The design is the easy part. Defining what "good" means is the hard part.

Show them you know the difference.

**CTA:**
What's the best "clarifying question" you've heard (or asked) in a system design round?

## Post 4: Your Past Career is a Differentiator, Not a Gap
**Target:** Jordan (The Career Switcher)
**Hook:** 3 years ago: High school teacher. Today: Senior engineer at a fintech startup.

**Body:**
When I started interviewing, I was terrified.
"Why should we hire someone without a CS degree?"
"Can you really compete with candidates from Stanford?"

I spent months apologizing for my background. Until I realized my non-tech career wasn't a liability. It was a superpower.

I stopped saying "I used to be a teacher" and started saying:
→ "I spent 8 years explaining complex concepts to skeptical audiences. That's what engineering communication is."
→ "I've debugged why 30 different students were confused about the same concept. That's what debugging code is."
→ "I managed 150 stakeholders with competing needs. That's what product development is."

If you're a career switcher, stop leading with the "gap." Lead with the perspective no one else in the room has.

**CTA:**
Are you transitioning into tech? What's one skill from your previous career that makes you a better engineer?

## Post 5: The 80/20 of Interview Prep
**Target:** All Personas (Efficiency)
**Hook:** I helped 200 engineers interview—here’s where they waste the most time.

**Body:**
Average time spent preparing for a FAANG interview: 150 hours.
70% of that time is usually spent on low-value activities.

If you have a full-time job, you don't have 150 hours. You need the 80/20.

**Lowest ROI:**
- Grinding random LeetCode problems without a pattern.
- Reading interview books without practicing out loud.
- Passive YouTube watching.

**Highest ROI:**
1. Master 5 Behavioral Stories: Behavioral rounds eliminate 60% of candidates who pass technical screens.
2. Pattern Recognition: master 20 core coding patterns, not 200 problems.
3. Realistic Mocks: Nothing substitutes for simulated pressure.

Behavioral prep is the fastest path to an offer. It’s where most engineers are weakest, and it’s where you can improve the most in the shortest time.

Quality practice beats quantity every time.

**CTA:**
If you had to pass an interview in 48 hours, what's the ONE thing you would focus on?
