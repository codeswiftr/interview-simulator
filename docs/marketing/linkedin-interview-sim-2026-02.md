## Post 1: The "Story Bank" Strategy (Behavioral Prep)
**Hook:**
Memorizing 50 different interview answers is a trap.
You only need 5 stories.

**Body:**
Engineers often over-prepare for behavioral rounds by scripting answers to every possible question.
"Tell me about a conflict." "Tell me about a failure." "Tell me about a technical challenge."
This leads to robotic delivery and panic when you get a curveball.

The Fix: Build a **Story Bank**.
Identify 5-7 "Hero Moments" from your career (e.g., The Big Migration, The Production Outage, The Team Disagreement).
Each of these stories can be "multi-threaded" to answer 10 different questions depending on which angle you emphasize.
Don't memorize scripts. Know your key career moments cold, and adapt them on the fly.

## Post 2: STAR 2.0 (Fixing the Framework)
**Hook:**
The STAR method is the industry standard.
It's also why your answers sound boring.

**Body:**
Most candidates spend 60 seconds on the "Situation" (Context) and 10 seconds on the "Action" (What they actually did).
The interviewer is asleep before you get to the good part.

Try **STAR 2.0**:
**S/T (Context):** Keep it under 15 seconds. High stakes only.
**A (Action):** This is 70% of your answer. Show your trade-offs, your reasoning, and your specific contribution.
**R (Result):** Quantify it. Numbers > Adjectives.
**L (Learned):** The "Senior" differentiator. What would you do differently next time?

Stop burying the lead. Start with the impact.

## Post 3: The 7 Deadly Sins of Tech Interviews
**Hook:**
You have the skills. You have the resume.
So why aren't you getting the offer?

**Body:**
It's often a blind spot you don't even know you have.
The "7 Deadly Sins" of modern tech interviews:
1.  **The "We" Trap:** Being too humble and not claiming your own work.
2.  **Monotone Delivery:** Low energy reads as disinterest over Zoom.
3.  **The Silent Coder:** Failing to "think out loud" during technical rounds.
4.  **Bad-Mouthing:** complaining about past employers (major red flag).
5.  **Ignoring Cues:** Missing hints from the interviewer.
6.  **The "Perfect" Lie:** Claiming you've never failed.
7.  **Bad Setup:** Poor audio/lighting = poor executive presence.

## Post 4: The Camera Eye Contact Hack (Remote Tips)
**Hook:**
You're making eye contact with the interviewer on your screen.
To them, you're looking at their tie.

**Body:**
Remote interviews have a geometry problem.
When you look at the face on the screen, your eyes are downcast. It looks like you're reading notes or checking your phone.
The "Presence Hack": Look at the **lens**, not the screen.
It feels unnatural. You're talking to a piece of glass.
But on the other side, it looks like confident, direct eye contact.
Combine this with 20% more vocal energy (to cut through the "video filter"), and you instantly stand out as a stronger communicator.

## Post 5: The "Thank You, But..." Script (Negotiation)
**Hook:**
The most expensive mistake engineers make?
Accepting the offer on the spot.

**Body:**
Negotiation isn't conflict. It's a business conversation.
But finding the right words is hard.
Use the "Thank You, But..." script:
"Thank you so much, I'm really excited about the team and the mission. However, I'm currently looking at a total compensation closer to [X] based on the market and my experience level. Is there any flexibility on the base or equity to bridge that gap?"
It's polite, firm, and opens the door for a 10-15% bump without risking the offer.
You don't get what you deserve; you get what you negotiate.
