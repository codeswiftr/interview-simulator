# Interview Simulator: FAANG Content Strategy

**Target Audience:** Mid-level software engineers (3-7 YOE) preparing for FAANG interviews  
**Goal:** Position Interview Simulator as the go-to practice platform for FAANG behavioral rounds  
**Tone:** Experienced peer mentor—insider knowledge, no BS, tactical advice  
**Last Updated:** February 5, 2026

---

## Target Persona: The FAANG Aspirant

**Profile:**
- **Experience:** 3-7 years at startups, mid-size companies, or non-FAANG tech firms
- **Current State:** Grinding LeetCode, nervous about behavioral rounds, unsure how to frame their experience
- **Motivation:** Career acceleration, compensation increase, prestige, learning opportunities
- **Pain Points:**
  - Behavioral rounds are a black box (what do FAANG actually look for?)
  - STAR method feels robotic and generic
  - Don't have "FAANG-worthy" stories from their current job
  - System design at scale is unfamiliar territory
  - Competition feels intense—thousands apply for every role

**What They Need:**
- Insider perspective on what interviewers actually evaluate
- Frameworks that go beyond STAR
- Ways to extract impressive stories from "boring" jobs
- Confidence that their preparation is working

## 5 Blog Post Topics

### 1. "What FAANG Behavioral Interviewers Actually Write in Their Notes"

**Hook:** Most candidates prepare the wrong things. Here's what interviewers are really scoring.

**Key Points:**
- The hidden evaluation rubric (leadership principles alignment, signal strength)
- Why "Tell me about a time you failed" is actually a test of ownership
- The difference between L4, L5, and L6 behavioral expectations
- Red flags that get candidates auto-rejected
- How interviewers calibrate against each other

**Angle:** Insider transparency—demystify the process  
**CTA:** "Practice with our FAANG-calibrated behavioral questions"  
**Estimated Traffic:** High (SEO target: "FAANG behavioral interview")

---

### 2. "The 'Scope & Impact' Framework: A Better Alternative to STAR for FAANG"

**Hook:** STAR is table stakes. FAANG interviewers expect you to communicate like a senior engineer.

**Key Points:**
- Why STAR feels rehearsed (and interviewers can tell)
- Introducing Scope & Impact: Context → Your Role → Technical Scope → Business Impact → Learning
- Before/after examples of the same story using both frameworks
- How to adjust depth based on interview level (30-second vs. 5-minute versions)
- Practice drills for each component

**Angle:** Upgrade their framework, not just their stories  
**CTA:** "Download the Scope & Impact Story Template"  
**Estimated Traffic:** Medium-High (shareable framework)

---

### 3. "I Worked at a Startup. Here's How I Passed Amazon's Bar Raiser Round."

**Hook:** You don't need FAANG experience to get hired by FAANG. You need FAANG-caliber stories.

**Key Points:**
- The candidate's background (startup full-stack dev, 4 YOE)
- The story extraction process: finding high-impact moments in "normal" work
- Reframing startup chaos as leadership principle alignment
- Specific preparation strategy for Amazon's unique culture
- Lessons learned and what they would do differently

**Angle:** Relatable success story from similar background  
**CTA:** "Get the Story Extraction Worksheet I used"  
**Estimated Traffic:** Medium (case study format)

---

### 4. "The 5 Stories Every Mid-Level Engineer Needs Before a FAANG Loop"

**Hook:** Don't prepare 50 stories. Prepare 5 that you can adapt to any question.

**Key Points:**
- The 5 essential story archetypes:
  1. **The Complex Technical Challenge** (depth, ownership, learning)
  2. **The Cross-Team Collaboration** (influence, communication, conflict)
  3. **The Big Mistake** (accountability, growth, customer obsession)
  4. **The Process/System You Built** (scalability, vision, execution)
  5. **The Time You Disagreed** (conviction, data-driven decisions, outcomes)
- How to map each to multiple leadership principles
- The "story spine" technique for on-the-fly adaptation
- Practice prompts for each archetype

**Angle:** Efficient preparation—quality over quantity  
**CTA:** "Practice delivering these stories with AI feedback"  
**Estimated Traffic:** High (practical checklist)

---

### 5. "Why Senior Engineers Fail FAANG Behavioral Rounds (And Mid-Level Devs Pass)"

**Hook:** Years of experience don't matter if you can't demonstrate the right behaviors.

**Key Points:**
- The humility trap: senior engineers who "manage" instead of "do"
- Why "I delegated that" is the wrong answer at L5
- The expectation gap: Staff+ behavioral bars are brutal
- What mid-level devs can learn from senior failures
- How to show growth potential without overstating

**Angle:** Counterintuitive take—experience isn't everything  
**CTA:** "Assess your readiness with our FAANG-level practice interviews"  
**Estimated Traffic:** Medium (controversial angle drives engagement)

## Appendix: Content Production Workflow

### Blog Post Template
1. Hook/problem statement (2-3 sentences)
2. Personal credibility/insider perspective (1 paragraph)
3. The "what" (explanation)
4. The "how" (framework/technique)
5. Examples (before/after)
6. Common mistakes
7. Action items
8. CTA

**Template A: Contrarian Take**
"Everyone says [common advice].
