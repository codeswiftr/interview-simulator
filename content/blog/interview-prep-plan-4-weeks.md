# 4-Week Technical Interview Prep Plan

Most interview preparation advice is either too vague ("just practice LeetCode") or too exhausting ("grind 3 problems a day every day"). This is a concrete 4-week plan designed for working engineers who can commit 1-2 hours per day on weekdays and 2-4 hours on weekends.

## Week 1: Assess and Build Foundations

**Goal**: Know where you are, close the most critical gaps.

**Days 1-2: Assessment**
- Solve 3 LeetCode problems without help: one easy, one medium, one hard
- Do one system design session (use a whiteboard or paper, set a 45-minute timer)
- Write out answers to: "Tell me about yourself," "Why are you looking?", "Biggest technical challenge you've faced"
- Identify your two weakest areas from this assessment

**Days 3-5: Coding foundations**
Focus on the data structures you are weakest on. For most engineers, this is: graphs, dynamic programming, and sliding window.
- Day 3: Arrays and strings — solve 3 problems focusing on two-pointer and sliding window patterns
- Day 4: Trees — 3 problems covering DFS, BFS, and level-order traversal
- Day 5: Graphs — 3 problems, graph as adjacency list, BFS for shortest path, DFS for connected components

**Weekend**: One full mock interview session (coding + system design back to back, 3 hours total). Use Pramp, interviewing.io, or a friend.

## Week 2: Deepen Patterns and Start System Design

**Goal**: Build pattern fluency in coding, start system design vocabulary.

**Days 1-2: More coding patterns**
- Day 1: Dynamic programming — classic problems: climbing stairs, coin change, longest common subsequence
- Day 2: Heaps — top K problems, median data stream
- Review your Week 1 mistakes — redo problems you got wrong or solved poorly

**Days 3-5: System design introduction**
Pick one system design topic per day and do a full 45-minute solo session:
- Day 3: Design a URL shortener (introduces hashing, key-value stores, redirect infrastructure)
- Day 4: Design a notification system (introduces fan-out, queues, delivery guarantees)
- Day 5: Design a rate limiter (introduces token bucket, leaky bucket, Redis patterns)

For each design: 5 min requirements, 10 min high-level design, 20 min detailed design, 10 min trade-offs and scaling discussion.

**Weekend**: Research your target companies. Read 2-3 engineering blog posts from each. Write one sentence summarizing what technical problem each company is known for solving.

## Week 3: Mock Interviews and Behavioral Prep

**Goal**: Simulate real interview conditions, build behavioral story bank.

**Days 1-2: Coding at interview pace**
Stop grinding new problems. Practice solving problems you have seen before *at interview pace*: explain out loud, code cleanly, handle edge cases, state complexity. Speed matters less than communication quality.

**Days 3-4: Behavioral story bank**
Write out 5-7 detailed STAR stories from your experience. Cover:
- Most complex technical problem you solved
- Time you made a technical decision others disagreed with (and how it turned out)
- Time something you built failed in production
- Biggest scope project you drove end-to-end
- Time you improved a team process or culture

Each story: 2 minutes when told aloud. Practice out loud — stories that read well often sound stiff.

**Day 5: System design — harder problems**
- Design a distributed cache
- Design a chat system (or review your notes on this)

**Weekend**: Two full mock interviews, ideally with different people. Get feedback. Don't try to fix everything — pick one thing to improve from each mock.

## Week 4: Target-Specific Prep and Recovery

**Goal**: Tailor preparation to your specific companies, reduce anxiety.

**Days 1-2: Company-specific preparation**
- Review Glassdoor interview reports for each company you have scheduled
- Identify 2-3 system design questions each company is known for
- Prepare a company-specific "why this company" answer that references something real about their engineering

**Days 3-4: Reduce intensity**
Do not add new material this week. Review your notes, redo 1-2 problems per day, and focus on the areas where you feel least confident. Over-preparing leads to fatigue.

**Day 5: Logistics and mental prep**
- Confirm interview format, timing, and setup
- Test your equipment (camera, microphone, internet, coding environment)
- Write down your 3 strongest behavioral stories and review them
- Get good sleep

**Weekend before interviews**: Light review only. Take a walk. Your brain needs consolidation time, not more content.

## Daily Routine That Works

**Morning (30 min)**: One coding problem — solve it before you check email. This primes your problem-solving brain and builds consistency.

**Evening (30-60 min)**: System design or behavioral practice. Less demanding than coding, good for the end of the day.

**Friday evening**: Review the week's mistakes. Identify the pattern you struggled with most. Spend 20 minutes on it.

## What to Skip

- **Hard LeetCode problems before mastering mediums**: Most interviews use mediums. Getting good at mediums is more valuable than occasionally solving hards.
- **Memorizing solutions**: Recognize patterns, not answers. A solution you cannot regenerate in an interview is useless.
- **Preparation you are not doing**: A plan you do not follow for 4 weeks is worse than a simpler plan you complete. Adjust the daily targets to what you will actually do.

## Signs You Are Ready

- You can identify the pattern in a new problem within 2 minutes
- You can talk through a system design for 45 minutes without running out of things to say
- You can deliver your "tell me about yourself" answer without stumbling
- You have at least 3 solid STAR stories ready

When you hit these four, you are as prepared as additional preparation will make you. Schedule the interviews.
