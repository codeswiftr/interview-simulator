# New Grad Software Engineer Interview Guide

Getting your first software engineering job is one of the most high-stakes interviews you'll face — it sets the trajectory for everything that follows. This guide gives you a strategy for the full process, from internship to full-time conversion and campus recruiting timelines.

## The Campus Recruiting Timeline

Most new grad recruiting at top tech companies starts much earlier than students expect:

**Summer internship recruiting:**
- **August–September (junior year)**: Big Tech companies (Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Apple) open applications. Apply immediately — spots fill fast.
- **October–November**: Most other tech companies open internship applications.
- **January–March**: Later-stage applications; many spots already filled, but still worth applying.

**Full-time new grad recruiting:**
- **August–September (senior year)**: Same cycle as internship, but for full-time roles. Having a return offer from your summer internship significantly simplifies this.
- **October–February**: Rolling applications; start-dates typically June–August.

**The single most important timeline advice**: Apply to Big Tech in August/September of your junior year for summer internships, and August/September of your senior year for full-time. Missing this window means competing for a much smaller pool of spots.

## The Internship → Full-Time Path

The most reliable path to a strong first full-time offer is through an internship at the target company. Return offer rates at top tech companies are typically 70–90% for interns who meet expectations.

**Internship performance tips:**
- Deliver your project completely — partial delivery is the most common reason interns don't get return offers
- Ask for feedback at the midpoint, not the end — you need time to course-correct
- Build relationships with your team — return offers aren't purely performance-based
- Make your contributions visible — write clear status updates, demo your work

## Coding Interview Preparation for New Grads

For new grads, coding interview preparation is the primary lever. You're being compared against hundreds of other candidates with similar experience levels — your differentiator is preparation quality.

### The 12-Week Plan

**Weeks 1–3: Foundations**
- Arrays, strings, hash maps — the 3 most common problem types
- Big-O notation — understand time and space complexity fluently
- Python or Java fluency — whichever you prefer, be fast in one language
- Target: 30 LeetCode easy problems

**Weeks 4–6: Core Patterns**
- Two pointers, sliding window
- Linked lists, stacks/queues
- Binary search
- Target: 40 LeetCode easy/medium problems

**Weeks 7–9: Advanced Structures**
- Trees (DFS/BFS, recursion)
- Graphs (adjacency list, BFS, DFS, cycle detection)
- Basic dynamic programming (memoization pattern)
- Target: 40 LeetCode medium problems

**Weeks 10–12: Consolidation**
- Mixed practice sessions (simulate real interview: 45 minutes, no hints)
- Mock interviews with friends or on Pramp/interviewing.io
- Review and re-solve problems you got wrong
- Target: 30 LeetCode medium/hard + 5 mock interviews

### LeetCode Strategy

- Don't solve problems randomly — use a structured list (Blind 75, Neetcode 150)
- When stuck for 20+ minutes, look at the solution, understand it fully, then solve it again from scratch a few days later
- Practice explaining your solution out loud, not just implementing it
- Track patterns, not just solutions — "this was another sliding window problem" is more valuable than memorizing a specific solution

## Building Your Portfolio

For new grads, projects matter. A strong portfolio can compensate for a lower GPA and can differentiate you from candidates with similar academic backgrounds.

**What makes a strong project:**
- **Real users**: A side project with actual users (even 50) demonstrates initiative and product thinking
- **Technical depth**: A project that uses something non-trivial — a custom data structure, a scalable backend, a real-time system
- **Completeness**: Finished projects > abandoned projects with great ambitions

**Project ideas that resonate with interviewers:**
- A web app with real functionality (not a todo app clone)
- A CLI tool that solves a real developer problem
- A data pipeline or ML model with real data
- An open-source contribution to a project you actually use

**GitHub hygiene**: Clean commits, good README files, and no secrets in history. Interviewers will look at your profile.

## Evaluating Your First Offer

For new grads, the most important factor in your first offer is often **not** the compensation — it's the quality of the engineering experience you'll get.

**What to optimize for:**
1. **Learning velocity**: Will you be writing production code quickly, or doing support work for 2 years?
2. **Manager quality**: Is your hiring manager someone who invests in junior engineers?
3. **Onboarding and mentorship**: Does the company have a structured new grad program?
4. **Code quality culture**: Will you learn good engineering practices or pick up bad habits?

**Compensation matters, but context matters more**: A $130K salary at a company where you'll ship production code in month 2 may be better for your career than $160K at a company where you'll maintain legacy systems for 3 years.

**FAANG vs. smaller companies for first jobs**: Both work. FAANG provides brand recognition, structured programs, and strong peers. Smaller companies often provide faster growth in scope and responsibility. Choose based on what you want to optimize.

## The First 90 Days

Getting the job is step one. Succeeding in the first 90 days is step two:
- Ship something in your first 30 days, even if small
- Ask your manager what "success in 90 days" looks like — then deliver it
- Build relationships across the team before you need help
- Ask questions aggressively early (it's expected from new grads) while documenting the answers

Your first job sets patterns that persist throughout your career. Choose it thoughtfully, prepare thoroughly, and invest in relationships that will matter long after you've moved on.
