# Prague Tech Job Guide: Central Europe's Rising Engineering Hub

Prague has quietly become one of Central Europe's most important tech hubs, attracting both established MNCs setting up engineering centers and a growing local startup ecosystem. With low cost of living, excellent quality of life, EU work authorization for EU citizens, and salaries that have risen significantly, Prague is increasingly on engineers' radars.

## The Market Structure

**Global MNC centers:** Oracle, Red Hat (IBM), Avast/Gen Digital, SAP, Cisco, ABB, Honeywell, Siemens, Komerční banka (Société Générale). These companies established Prague offices for the talent pool and cost arbitrage. Engineering roles do real product work, not just maintenance. Salaries are below Western Europe but among the highest in the Czech market.

**Czech tech companies:** Avast (acquired by NortonLifeLock/Gen Digital — Prague origin), Kiwi.com (travel tech), Productboard (product management SaaS, US-funded unicorn, Prague-founded), Kentico (CMS), Rossum (document AI), Mews (hospitality tech). Several Czech-founded companies have reached unicorn or near-unicorn status.

**Startups:** Growing seed and Series A ecosystem, many EU-funded. Czechia's startup funding has grown 10× in 5 years. English is the working language at international startups.

## Salary Benchmarks (2026)

Czech Republic uses CZK (Czech Koruna). At ~24 CZK/EUR (2026 rates):

| Level | Czech Company | MNC Center |
|-------|---------------|------------|
| Junior (0-2 yrs) | 45-65K CZK/mo | 55-75K CZK/mo |
| Mid (2-5 yrs) | 65-95K CZK/mo | 80-110K CZK/mo |
| Senior (5-8 yrs) | 95-140K CZK/mo | 120-160K CZK/mo |
| Staff/Lead | 140-190K CZK/mo | 160-220K CZK/mo |

Converting: 120K CZK/month ≈ €5,000/month ≈ €60K/year gross. After Czech income tax (15-23%) and social contributions (~11%), net is approximately 75-80% of gross.

For comparison, a senior engineer in Amsterdam or Munich earns €80-120K but pays €2,000-3,000/month rent. A senior in Prague earns €60-75K but pays €800-1,200/month rent. Purchasing power parity is comparable or better.

## Work Authorization

**EU/EEA citizens:** Full work rights in Czech Republic. No permit required, same as Czech nationals.

**Non-EU with EU work experience:** EU Blue Card is the standard pathway — requires job offer above salary threshold (typically €1.5× average salary) and recognized tertiary education. Processing: 3-4 months.

**Czech Employee Card:** Work permit for non-EU workers. Requires job offer from Czech employer. Takes 30-90 days.

**Remote work from Prague:** EU citizens with foreign employer income need to register for Czech health insurance and social security if residing in Czech Republic for 183+ days/year. Non-EU citizens need work authorization tied to Czech employment.

## Key Neighborhoods for Tech Workers

**Žižkov / Vinohrady / Vršovice:** Central, lively, mix of locals and expats. Prague 2/3 postal codes. Popular with young professionals. 1-bedroom apartments: €600-900/month.

**Smíchov:** Developing tech district, several startup offices, newer residential buildings, good metro connectivity.

**Holešovice:** Industrial heritage, converted to creative offices and coworking spaces. Several tech companies have offices here. Good café culture.

**Prague 6 (Bubeneč / Dejvice):** Quieter, more residential, popular with families. Near Czech Technical University (ČVUT).

## The Tech Stack Landscape

Prague's tech community is polyglot. Java and .NET are strong in enterprise and MNC work. Python has grown significantly in the startup scene. JavaScript/TypeScript for full-stack roles. Go is popular in DevOps and infrastructure roles.

Certifications (AWS, Azure) carry real weight for cloud engineering positions — more so than in some Western markets where practical portfolio matters more. Czech engineers often have strong theoretical computer science foundations from ČVUT and Masaryk University.

## Hiring Process

Czech interview processes vary considerably:
- MNC centers typically use their headquarters' processes (coding rounds, system design, behavioral)
- Czech product companies: technical assessment (take-home or live coding), 2-3 interview rounds
- Enterprise/consultancy: CV screening, brief technical questions, culture fit

English is universally required for international companies and increasingly for Czech companies. Czech language is beneficial for larger local companies (Komerční banka, ČEZ digital) but not required at most international-facing organizations.

## Cost of Living

Prague is affordable by Western European standards:
- 1-bedroom apartment in central Prague: €700-1,100/month
- Monthly groceries: €250-350
- Public transit pass: €25/month (excellent tram/metro coverage)
- Eating out: €8-15 for a good restaurant meal
- Total comfortable monthly spend: €1,800-2,800

Healthcare: Czech public health insurance is mandatory and comprehensive. Employer and employee both contribute. Quality of care is generally good.

## Community and Networking

Prague has active Python, JavaScript, and Go meetup communities. PYVO (Python meetups), Node.js Prague, Golang Prague, and cloud computing meetups are well-attended. WebExpo (annual web conference) and various DevOps/cloud events attract regional attendance.

The expat tech community is concentrated in Vinohrady and Žižkov — there are informal Slack communities and coworking spaces that serve as gathering points.
