# The Best Software Engineering Interview Prep Resources in 2025

The software engineering interview prep industry has exploded. There are now dozens of platforms, books, courses, and communities competing for your time and money. Most candidates either over-invest in one type of preparation (usually LeetCode grinding) while neglecting others, or spend so much time evaluating resources that they never actually prepare.

This guide cuts through the noise. It covers the best resources by category, notes where free options match or exceed paid ones, and explains how to combine them into a coherent prep strategy.

## Coding Practice: Algorithms and Data Structures

**LeetCode** remains the standard for algorithm interview preparation. Its problem library is the largest, its company-tagged problem lists are valuable for targeted preparation, and its discussion sections often contain better explanations than any textbook. The free tier covers most problems; premium ($35/month or $159/year) unlocks company-specific problem lists, a question timer, and editorial solutions for all problems.

For most candidates, the free tier is sufficient. Focus on the curated "Blind 75" list (widely shared on the Blind forum) or the "NeetCode 150"—both are subsets of LeetCode problems chosen to cover all major pattern types efficiently.

**HackerRank** is weaker than LeetCode for interview prep but worth knowing because many companies use HackerRank's platform to administer their own take-home coding assessments. Familiarity with the interface and its IDE quirks reduces friction on test day.

**AlgoExpert** ($99–$149 one-time) offers 160 hand-curated problems with high-quality video solutions from ex-FAANG engineers. It is better structured than LeetCode for beginners—problems are organized by category and difficulty, and the video explanations are clearer than most LeetCode editorials. The trade-off: the problem library is much smaller, so it cannot replace LeetCode for breadth.

**NeetCode.io** offers free video solutions for the NeetCode 150 list and a structured roadmap. The production quality of the videos is high and the explanations are unusually clear. For candidates who learn better through video than text, NeetCode's free content may be the best algorithm resource available.

## System Design: Books and Courses

**"Designing Data-Intensive Applications" by Martin Kleppmann** is the best technical book for system design interview preparation—and one of the best software engineering books written in the past decade. It covers distributed systems fundamentals, databases, replication, partitioning, consistency, and stream processing with exceptional depth and clarity. It is not an interview prep book; it is a serious technical resource that makes you a better engineer. Reading it takes weeks, but it builds the durable understanding that carries through staff-level system design interviews.

**"System Design Interview" by Alex Xu (Volumes 1 and 2)** takes the opposite approach: it is explicitly an interview prep book, organized as a series of case studies (design a URL shortener, design Twitter, design a notification system). Each chapter walks through a structured approach: scope the problem, estimate scale, design data flow, then drill into components. Volume 1 covers foundational systems; Volume 2 covers more advanced cases. Both volumes are practical and efficient for candidates with limited prep time.

**Grokking the System Design Interview** (available on Educative.io, ~$79/year for platform access) covers similar ground to Alex Xu's books in an interactive format with embedded diagrams. The content is solid but the platform's pricing model—everything requires a subscription—makes it harder to recommend over the books, which you own permanently. It is worth a trial month if you prefer interactive learning.

**ByteByteGo** (Alex Xu's companion site, ~$79/year) provides animated system design explanations and a newsletter with new case studies weekly. Useful as a supplement for candidates who want ongoing exposure to new system design problems.

## Mock Interviews: Practice Under Real Conditions

Reading and practicing on your own is insufficient preparation for the pressure of a real technical interview. Mock interviews with a human interviewer—where you must think out loud, handle ambiguous questions, and maintain coherent reasoning while coding—are irreplaceable.

**Pramp** offers free peer-to-peer mock interviews. You interview someone else, then they interview you, using curated problems provided by the platform. The matching quality is inconsistent—your practice partner may be more or less experienced than you—but the price (free) makes it accessible to everyone. Best used for practice volume, not for calibration.

**interviewing.io** matches you with experienced engineers from top companies for anonymous mock interviews. After the interview, you receive recorded video with detailed feedback. Pricing ranges from $100 to $225 per session depending on the interviewer's seniority. The quality is consistently high and the feedback is specific. It is the best resource available if you can afford it, especially for candidates preparing for FAANG-level roles. The platform also posts anonymized interview recordings publicly, which are worth watching even without booking a session.

**IGotAnOffer** focuses on system design and product management interviews, with coaches who are former FAANG interviewers. Sessions run $150–$300. For staff and principal-level system design preparation, working with a coach who has sat on the other side of that exact type of interview is highly efficient.

**Meetapro** and **Prepfully** offer similar services at varying price points and are worth comparing if interviewing.io's availability or pricing does not work for you.

## Behavioral Preparation

Behavioral interviews are underestimated by technical candidates and often determine the outcome for senior roles. The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline framework—but strong candidates go further, tailoring their stories to the specific leadership principles or competencies the company publishes.

**"Cracking the PM Interview" by Gayle Laakmann McDowell** has a behavioral section that is more useful than its title suggests, even for software engineers. McDowell's behavioral question analysis is thorough and the story-crafting framework is practical.

**Company-specific prep** matters more than most candidates realize. Amazon's Leadership Principles, Google's four hiring criteria, and Microsoft's growth mindset framing each require different story selection and emphasis. Read the company's published values, engineering blog, and recent all-hands materials to understand what they signal internally.

## Free vs Paid: How to Choose

The free resources have never been better. NeetCode's algorithm videos, "Designing Data-Intensive Applications" (purchasable for ~$50 but available through many library systems), and Pramp together constitute a complete interview prep program at no cost.

Paid resources make sense in specific situations: AlgoExpert for candidates who want structured progression without navigating LeetCode's sprawling library; interviewing.io sessions for candidates interviewing at top companies who need calibrated feedback; Alex Xu's books for efficient system design preparation on a time budget.

The optimal combination: use NeetCode's roadmap and LeetCode for algorithms, read Alex Xu's Volume 1 alongside Kleppmann's book for system design, and book two to three interviewing.io sessions in the final two weeks of preparation. Total cost: approximately $150–$300 in mock interviews, $50 in books. Total time: eight to twelve weeks for a thorough preparation. The return on that investment, given the salary differential between prepared and unprepared candidates, is exceptional.
