# How to Study LeetCode Effectively

The average LeetCode grinder solves 200+ problems, burns out, and still freezes in interviews. The effective LeetCode studier solves 150 problems deliberately and walks into interviews confident. The difference is strategy.

## The Patterns-First Approach

Most candidates study LeetCode problem-by-problem, treating each as an isolated puzzle. This doesn't build the pattern recognition that matters in real interviews.

The better approach: **organize your study by pattern, not by problem**.

**The 15 core patterns that cover ~80% of interview problems:**

1. Arrays and hashing (frequency count, complement lookup)
2. Two pointers (sorted arrays, palindromes)
3. Sliding window (substring problems, subarray problems)
4. Stack and monotonic stack (matching, next greater element)
5. Binary search (sorted arrays, search on answer space)
6. Linked list (two pointers, in-place reversal)
7. Trees: DFS recursive (path problems, tree construction)
8. Trees: BFS (level order, shortest path in unweighted tree)
9. Heap / priority queue (top K, merge K sorted, median)
10. Intervals (merge overlapping, insert interval)
11. Greedy (locally optimal choices)
12. Backtracking (permutations, subsets, constraint satisfaction)
13. Dynamic programming (1D, 2D, knapsack, string DP)
14. Graphs: DFS/BFS (components, cycle detection, topological sort)
15. Graphs: Dijkstra / Union-Find (weighted shortest path, components)

For each pattern, study 5–10 representative problems, understand the template, then move to the next pattern.

## How Many Problems You Actually Need

The commonly cited number is 500+ problems. This is overkill for most candidates.

**The actual targets by company tier:**

| Target | Problems Needed | Time Required |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| Tier 1 (FAANG, Stripe, etc.) | 150–200 deliberate | 8–12 weeks |
| Tier 2 (strong tech companies) | 100–150 | 6–8 weeks |
| Tier 3 (most tech companies) | 75–100 | 4–6 weeks |

More important than the number: **problem quality and review quality**.

## The Problem-Solving Protocol

For each problem you study, follow this protocol:

1. **Read** — understand the problem fully, don't start coding
2. **Think (15–20 minutes max)** — try to solve it without looking at solutions
3. **If stuck** — look at a hint, not the full solution; try again for 10 more minutes
4. **Study the solution** — if you couldn't solve it, study the optimal solution until you understand *why* it works
5. **Implement from memory** — close the solution and re-implement it
6. **Analyze** — what pattern is this? What made you recognize (or miss) it?
7. **Review in 3 days and 1 week** — spaced repetition is essential

Most candidates skip step 5 (implementing from memory) and step 7 (review). These are the most important steps.

## The Neetcode 150 vs. Blind 75

**Blind 75**: A well-known curated list of 75 high-signal problems. Good starting point; covers all key patterns; can be completed in 4–6 weeks.

**Neetcode 150**: An extension with better categorization and video explanations. Recommended for anyone targeting Tier 1 companies.

**NeetCode.io** also provides a structured roadmap with video solutions — the best free resource for structured LeetCode study.

## Tracking Progress

Use a spreadsheet or Notion to track each problem with:
- Problem name and difficulty
- Pattern category
- Status: Unsolved / Needed hint / Solved independently / Review needed
- Date first solved
- Next review date

Color-code by confidence. Your goal is to move every problem to "Solved independently" — not just to have solved it once.

## Common Study Mistakes

**Solving on easy mode**: If you look at the title, related tags, and "Similar questions" before attempting the problem, you're solving with massive hints. Practice in "contest mode" (no tags, no hints) regularly.

**Moving on too quickly**: Finishing a problem once isn't the same as knowing it. Problems should be reviewed until you can solve them independently from a cold start.

**Ignoring hard problems**: Many Tier 1 company questions are LeetCode hard. If you never practice hard problems, they'll blindside you. Aim for 20–30 hard problems in your study plan.

**Over-indexing on one language**: Practice Python for speed, but be able to code in a second language (Java, C++) for companies with language preferences.

**Neglecting time pressure**: Solving problems with no time limit feels very different from interview conditions. Once you're comfortable with a pattern, practice solving within 30 minutes per problem.

## The Final Two Weeks

Two weeks before interviews, shift from learning to drilling:

- 4–6 mock interview sessions (Pramp, interviewing.io, or with a friend)
- Review every problem you previously struggled with
- Practice verbalizing your solutions out loud — silence is the enemy in interviews
- Stop learning new patterns; consolidate what you know

LeetCode is a means to an end — the end being that you can solve novel algorithmic problems under pressure while communicating clearly. Study with that goal in mind, and the grind becomes much more purposeful.
