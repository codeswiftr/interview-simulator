# Lagos Nigeria Advanced Tech Guide

Lagos is not just Nigeria's commercial capital — it is arguably Africa's most consequential technology city. With a population exceeding 20 million, a GDP that would rank it among mid-sized African nations if it were its own country, and a fintech ecosystem that has produced global companies, Lagos has earned its place on the map of international engineering talent. This advanced guide goes beyond surface-level advice.

## Why Lagos Is Different From Other African Tech Hubs

Nairobi has M-Pesa and MPESA-driven mobile money innovation. Cape Town has investment capital and quality of life. But Lagos has scale — raw, chaotic, generative scale. The density of economic activity, the size of the unbanked population, and the entrepreneurial intensity creates problems that *have* to be solved and a market large enough to sustain the solutions.

Nigeria has ~40 million unbanked or underbanked adults. Solving payments, credit scoring, and financial access for that population has created some of Africa's most valuable technology companies — and shaped an engineering culture that prioritizes pragmatism, resilience, and user empathy over theoretical elegance.

## The Fintech Ecosystem: Deep Dive

### Flutterwave

Founded in 2016 by Iyinoluwa Aboyeji and Olugbenga Agboola, Flutterwave became Africa's most valuable startup (valued at $3B+) by building payment infrastructure for businesses across Africa and diaspora corridors. Their engineering stack involves:
- **API-first design**: Flutterwave's core product is a payment API — engineers there live and breathe API ergonomics
- **Multi-currency and multi-rail**: Supporting 30+ currencies and integrations with local payment rails (NIBSS in Nigeria, M-Pesa in Kenya, BLIK in Poland for diaspora) is a serious distributed systems challenge
- **Compliance at scale**: PCI-DSS, CBN (Central Bank of Nigeria) regulations, and multi-jurisdiction licensing create a compliance engineering layer that rivals any fintech globally

**Interview focus at Flutterwave:** Payment reconciliation systems, idempotent payment APIs, distributed transaction integrity, and multi-currency ledgering.

### Paystack (Stripe-Acquired)

Paystack was acquired by Stripe in 2020 for ~$200M — the most significant fintech acquisition in African tech history. It continues to operate semi-independently and hires engineering talent in Lagos and remotely. Their developer-experience-first philosophy aligns with Stripe's DNA.

**Interview focus at Paystack:** API design, webhook reliability, fraud detection, developer tooling.

### The Broader Ecosystem

- **Opay**: Chinese-Nigerian neobank with 30M+ users, aggressive engineering hiring
- **Moniepoint** (formerly TeamApt): B2B payments for SMEs, raised $110M Series C
- **Cowrywise and Piggyvest**: Consumer savings and investment platforms — fintech meets behavioral economics
- **Bamboo and Chaka**: Cross-border stock trading — regulatory complexity meets retail finance
- **54gene**: African genomics — where biotech meets data engineering

## Salary Benchmarks (2025–2026)

Salaries are in NGN (Nigerian Naira). The exchange rate has been volatile (approximately ₦1,500–₦1,700 per USD in 2025–2026 following the CBN float).

| Role | Junior | Mid | Senior |
|------|--------|-----|--------|
| Software Engineer | ₦4M–₦8M/yr | ₦9M–₦18M/yr | ₦20M–₦40M/yr |
| Data/ML Engineer | ₦5M–₦9M/yr | ₦10M–₦20M/yr | ₦22M–₦45M/yr |
| DevOps/SRE | ₦5M–₦10M/yr | ₦11M–₦22M/yr | ₦24M–₦50M/yr |

**The USD conversation:** Many Lagos tech companies — especially those with international investors — pay senior engineers in USD or peg salaries to USD. This has become a major retention mechanism as the naira has depreciated. When negotiating, always clarify: is the salary in NGN or USD-pegged?

Remote roles for international companies (US, UK, EU) pay international rates. For a senior engineer earning $150,000 USD remotely while living in Lagos, the effective purchasing power is extraordinary relative to local costs of living.

## Engineering Culture: What Actually Happens in the Room

**"Lagos optimism" is real — and valuable.** The city selects for people who can execute under adverse conditions. Power cuts, internet instability, and infrastructure gaps mean Lagos engineers routinely build with resilience assumptions baked in from day one. This is an asset in global engineering roles — interviewers familiar with Lagos often cite this as a genuine differentiator.

**Community is everything.** The Lagos tech scene is held together by WhatsApp groups, Twitter/X threads (Nigeria punches above its weight on tech Twitter), and communities like **HNG Internship**, **Ingressive For Good**, and **TECLABS**. Relationships formed in these spaces translate directly to job opportunities.

**Hustle culture with depth.** The reputation for "Nigeria hustle" can be a double-edged sword in interviews. Channel it into concrete examples of shipping under constraints rather than letting it manifest as over-promising.

**Pragmatism over purity.** Lagos engineers tend to solve problems quickly with what's available rather than waiting for ideal conditions. In interviews, frame this as resourcefulness — not corner-cutting.

## Visa, Remote Work, and the Diaspora Strategy

### Working Remotely from Lagos

The global remote work shift has been transformative for Lagos engineers. Platforms actively sourcing Lagos talent:
- **Andela**: Still a major pathway into international engineering roles
- **Toptal and Turing**: Remote marketplace with strong Nigerian representation
- **Deel and Remote.com**: Legal infrastructure for international employment
- **YC-backed startups**: YC has made deliberate efforts to fund Nigerian founders and hire Nigerian engineers

### Working Internationally

For Lagos engineers seeking to relocate:
- **UK Skilled Worker Visa**: Strong pipeline — UK specifically recognizes Nigerian engineering credentials. The Nigeria-UK Renewed Partnership has eased some pathways.
- **Canada Express Entry**: Tech roles score well; Nigerian applicants have had strong acceptance rates in recent years.
- **Portugal Digital Nomad Visa**: Popular for Nigerian diaspora; EU access after 5 years
- **Germany Opportunity Card**: New in 2024, allows skilled workers to enter Germany and job-search in person

**Important:** The Nigerian diaspora (estimated 1.7 million in the US alone) is a recruiting network. Nigerian-founded companies in the US, UK, and Canada actively seek Lagos engineering talent.

## Preparing for Lagos Company Interviews

### Technical Preparation

1. **Payment systems internals**: Know how NIBSS (Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System) works conceptually, how card processing flows, and what "settlement" means in a multi-bank context.
2. **Offline-first architecture**: Mobile money in low-connectivity environments requires offline capability, eventual consistency, and robust sync protocols.
3. **Anti-fraud at scale**: Nigerian fintech deals with sophisticated fraud (card fraud, account takeover, identity fraud). Know behavioral analytics, velocity checks, and device fingerprinting.
4. **High-throughput systems**: A neobank serving 30 million users needs serious backend engineering. Know horizontal scaling, database sharding, and queue-based architectures.

### Cultural Preparation

5. **Know the companies deeply.** Read Flutterwave's engineering blog, Paystack's developer documentation, and news about Nigerian fintech regulation (CBN policies matter enormously).
6. **Be specific about Nigeria.** Vague "I'm excited about African fintech" answers don't land. Know that Nigeria processes more mobile money by volume than any African country except Kenya.
7. **Demonstrate resilience stories.** "I built X while dealing with Y constraint" resonates strongly with Lagos interviewers who've been there.

## The Future: What's Coming

Lagos tech in 2026–2028 will be shaped by:
- **AI adoption**: Nigerian startups are among the fastest adopters of LLM APIs for localization, fraud detection, and customer service
- **Digital public infrastructure**: Nigeria's National Identity Number (NIN) system maturing creates identity verification APIs — watch for a wave of identity-layer startups
- **Manufacturing tech**: Nigeria's push to reduce oil dependence is driving industrial IoT and supply chain tech investment

Lagos rewards engineers who see beyond the chaos to the underlying opportunity. If you can build for Lagos, you can build for anywhere.
