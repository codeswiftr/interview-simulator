# Istanbul Tech Interview Guide: Turkey's Largest Tech Hub

Istanbul is Turkey's technology capital and one of the most dynamic emerging tech markets in the broader Europe-Middle East region. The city hosts global tech companies, a growing unicorn ecosystem, and a population of 15 million with rising digital adoption. For engineers, Istanbul offers high salaries by Turkish standards, a vibrant startup community, and increasingly, pathways to remote work for European and global companies.

## Istanbul's Tech Landscape

Turkey's tech scene is concentrated in Istanbul, with Ankara playing a secondary role in defense tech and government IT. Istanbul's ecosystem spans e-commerce, fintech, gaming, and enterprise software.

**Major domestic companies:** Trendyol (e-commerce, Alibaba subsidiary, Turkey's largest unicorn), Hepsiburada (e-commerce), Getir (ultrafast delivery, global expansion), Flo (mobile gaming, NASDAQ-listed), Akinon (e-commerce platform), Colendi (fintech), and Insider (marketing analytics, international SaaS). These are the flagship employers for Turkish engineers.

**Global presence:** Google has a significant Istanbul office. Amazon Web Services, Microsoft, SAP, and Siemens have engineering teams. Gaming companies (Zynga acquired Peak Games for $1.8B, Scopely acquired Gram Games) have validated Istanbul as a serious mobile gaming hub.

**Startups:** The Istanbul startup ecosystem has produced several unicorns and attracted significant VC investment. Startup Istanbul, 212, and RE-PIE are active investors. The ecosystem is younger than Western European equivalents but growing rapidly.

## Salaries and Compensation

Turkey's high inflation (historically 50-80% annually in recent years) creates complexity around compensation. Most senior engineers at international companies negotiate in USD or EUR to hedge against currency risk.

**Domestic company salaries (TRY-denominated, benchmarked against USD):** Mid-level software engineer: $1,500–2,500/month equivalent. Senior engineer: $2,500–4,000/month equivalent. Staff/principal: $4,000–6,000/month equivalent at top domestic companies.

**International company salaries:** Google, Amazon, Microsoft offer significantly higher compensation, often benchmarked to global bands, typically $4,000–8,000/month equivalent for senior roles.

**Remote salaries:** Many Turkish engineers work remotely for European or US companies, earning European-range salaries while benefiting from Turkey's lower cost of living. This is increasingly the highest-compensation path.

**Equity:** Turkish startup equity has produced real exits (Trendyol, Peak Games). Increasingly common at well-funded startups.

## The Interview Process

**Domestic tech companies:** Interview processes vary significantly in rigor. Trendyol and Getir have adopted structured technical processes with LeetCode-style screening and system design rounds, similar to international standards. Smaller companies often have lighter processes with more emphasis on cultural fit and portfolio work.

**International companies:** Google, Amazon, and Microsoft use their global standard processes. Expect LeetCode medium-hard, system design, and behavioral rounds. Preparation requirements identical to any global FAANG application.

**Startups:** Often portfolio-focused — GitHub profile, past projects, and references from the tight-knit Istanbul tech community matter more than algorithmic tests.

**Language:** English is used at international companies. Turkish is required at domestically-focused companies. Many engineers code-switch fluidly; covering both in a resume helps.

## Key Employers and Their Cultures

**Trendyol:** Turkey's most prestigious domestic tech employer. Fast-growing, data-driven, high expectations. Engineering culture influenced by Alibaba since acquisition. Strong system design emphasis; interviews involve high-scale e-commerce problems (inventory management, recommendation systems, logistics).

**Getir:** Ultrafast delivery startup with global operations. High pressure, rapid iteration. Engineering focus: real-time logistics, mobile, and supply chain optimization.

**Flo / Peak Games (Scopely):** Mobile gaming leaders. Gaming-specific algorithms (simulation, matchmaking, data analytics), alongside standard SWE interviews. High compensation by Istanbul standards due to global acquisition.

**Insider:** International SaaS company (marketing analytics). Strong engineering culture, English-speaking environment, distributed team. Interview process similar to mid-tier US SaaS companies.

## The Remote Engineering Opportunity

Istanbul's most significant economic opportunity for engineers is remote work. Turkish engineers with strong English and FAANG-adjacent skills routinely earn €5,000–10,000/month from European or US employers while benefiting from Istanbul's much lower living costs.

Platforms: LinkedIn (international roles), Toptal, Turing, and Arc.dev explicitly target Turkish engineers for US clients. Deel and Remote handle international payroll.

**Tax and legal:** Turkey has freelancer-friendly rules for engineers earning from abroad — exchange income (converting foreign currency to TRY) may qualify for advantageous tax treatment. Consult a Turkish tax advisor; the regulations change frequently.

## Tech Community

**Events:** Istanbul Tech Summit (annual flagship conference), various Google Developer Groups (GDG) chapters, AWS and Azure user groups, and the Turkish Python / JavaScript / Go meetups. The tech community is tight-knit — networking is effective.

**Education pipeline:** Boğaziçi, METU (Ankara), Bilkent, and Koç universities produce strong CS graduates, many of whom stay in Istanbul or return after international experience. Engineering talent density is high relative to Istanbul's position in the global tech hierarchy.

## Practical Considerations

Istanbul's European and Asian sides are connected by bridges and a metro line, but the city is large. Most tech offices cluster in Maslak, Levent (European side) and Ataşehir, Maltepe (Asian side). Commute planning matters.

Cost of living remains moderate by European standards despite inflation — housing, food, and transport are substantially cheaper than comparable European cities, even accounting for currency depreciation.

## Building a Strong Application

- Strong open source presence or portfolio projects — the community is small enough that reputation matters
- Experience with high-scale systems (Trendyol handles millions of transactions daily)
- Mobile development skills (gaming + e-commerce both have strong mobile demand)
- Competitive programming background valued at top companies
- English fluency signals international experience, which is prized
