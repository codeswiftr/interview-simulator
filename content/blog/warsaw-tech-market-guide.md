# Warsaw Tech Market Guide: Software Engineering Opportunities in Poland

Warsaw has become one of Central and Eastern Europe's premier tech destinations. With a combination of top-tier engineering universities, competitive salaries by European standards, and a growing density of both local unicorns and multinational R&D centers, it's a market worth understanding whether you're relocating or preparing to interview remotely.

## Why Warsaw?

Poland produces some of Europe's strongest engineering talent. Warsaw's technical universities — Warsaw University of Technology and the University of Warsaw — are consistently ranked among the best in the region. This talent base has attracted major investment from global technology companies.

**Major employers include:**
- **Global tech giants:** Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Samsung, and Goldman Sachs all have significant engineering centers in Warsaw. These offices work on real product development — not just support functions.
- **Polish unicorns and scale-ups:** Allegro (Poland's dominant e-commerce platform), CD Projekt Red (gaming), DocPlanner, and Brainly are headquartered here.
- **Fintech and banking tech:** mBank, ING Tech Poland, and Revolut's Warsaw office represent a strong financial technology cluster.
- **Outsourcing and consulting:** Companies like Accenture, Capgemini, and EPAM have large delivery centers in Warsaw — a major employer for mid-level engineers.

## Salary Landscape

Polish software engineering salaries have grown significantly over the last decade. As of 2026:

- Junior engineers: 8,000–13,000 PLN/month net (B2B contract)
- Mid-level engineers: 14,000–22,000 PLN/month net
- Senior engineers: 22,000–35,000 PLN/month net
- Staff/Principal: 35,000–55,000 PLN/month net

Most experienced engineers work on **B2B contracts** (działalność gospodarcza) rather than employment contracts, which provides tax efficiency. Total compensation at Warsaw offices of FAANG-adjacent companies approaches Western European levels, though equity packages remain smaller than in San Francisco or London.

Remote work paying in EUR or USD is common and highly attractive — many Warsaw-based engineers earn international rates while benefiting from Poland's lower cost of living.

## Interview Culture in Warsaw

Polish tech interviews are pragmatic and thorough. Here's what to expect at different company types:

### Global Tech Companies (Google, Amazon, Microsoft)
Standard global interview loops apply — LeetCode-style algorithmic problems, system design rounds, and behavioral interviews. Prepare exactly as you would for their US offices. The bar is the same.

### Polish Product Companies (Allegro, DocPlanner)
Expect a mix of practical and conceptual questions. Allegro in particular is known for rigorous technical interviews that emphasize distributed systems knowledge — their scale (millions of daily active users) means they care deeply about eventual consistency, service reliability, and caching strategies.

Take-home projects are common at mid-sized Polish startups. They tend to evaluate code quality, test coverage, and documentation over raw algorithmic complexity.

### Consulting and Outsourcing Firms
Interviews tend to be lighter on algorithm puzzles and heavier on communication, project management, and technology stack breadth. Client-facing skills are often explicitly evaluated.

## Technical Focus Areas

Warsaw's market heavily emphasizes backend and infrastructure roles, reflecting the R&D center nature of many offices:

- **Java and Kotlin** dominate at financial and e-commerce companies
- **Python** is strong in data engineering and ML teams
- **Go** is growing in infrastructure and platform teams
- **React/TypeScript** is standard for frontend positions
- **Cloud (AWS/GCP/Azure)** certifications and experience are increasingly expected at senior levels

## Practical Tips for Warsaw Interviews

**Learn the Polish business context.** If interviewing at Allegro or another local company, understand the Polish e-commerce and consumer market. Showing awareness of local competitive dynamics impresses Polish interviewers.

**Brush up on distributed systems.** Warsaw engineering centers often own complex, distributed systems. Questions about CAP theorem, database consistency models, and microservice communication patterns are common.

**Prepare for Polish directness.** Polish interview culture tends to be direct. Don't expect excessive small talk — interviewers appreciate concise, technically precise answers. This doesn't mean be terse; it means be substantive.

**English proficiency is assumed at tech companies.** Nearly all Warsaw tech interviews at international companies are conducted in English. Polish-language interviews are common at local companies and consulting firms.

**Understand the B2B contract norms.** When negotiating compensation, be prepared to discuss whether you prefer a B2B contract (invoice-based) or employment contract (umowa o pracę) and understand the tradeoffs.

## Living and Working in Warsaw

Warsaw is a liveable, affordable European capital. Public transportation is excellent, the tech community is active (meet.js Warsaw, Warsaw.rb, PyWaw), and English is widely spoken in tech circles.

Poland's EU membership means free movement for EU citizens and straightforward work permits for many non-EU engineers through the EU Blue Card scheme. The city continues to grow as a tech destination — getting in early means growing with a market that still has significant upside.
