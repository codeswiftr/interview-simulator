# Linear Software Engineer Interview Guide 2025

Linear is one of the most talked-about engineering cultures in the startup world. Built as an issue tracker for software teams, it has become the tool of choice for thousands of high-performance engineering organizations — and the team that builds it is held to the same standard. Linear is small by design, deeply product-focused, and obsessed with craft. For engineers who want to do their best work in an environment that genuinely values quality, it is a rare find.

## Linear's Engineering Culture

The foundational principle at Linear is that **speed and quality are not in tension — they reinforce each other**. Linear's product is fast. Obsessively fast. Interactions feel instant, navigation is keyboard-driven, and the interface never makes users wait. This is not accidental: it is the result of engineering decisions made at every layer of the stack, from data sync to rendering.

The team practices a **product-engineer ethos** where engineers are expected to own their features end-to-end. There are no separate product managers handing down specifications. Engineers define what to build, build it, and are accountable for the outcome. This requires strong product intuition, the ability to talk to users, and comfort with ambiguity.

Linear is also a **small team by choice**. The company has deliberately kept headcount low, which means every hire carries significant weight. Engineers at Linear work on problems that would be spread across multiple teams at a larger company, and they are expected to move quickly without sacrificing quality. Slow is not acceptable. Sloppy is not acceptable.

The culture is often described as **opinionated and direct**. Linear has a clear vision of what good software looks like, and the team operates with a shared aesthetic. If you prefer consensus-building on every decision, Linear is not the right fit. If you want to work with people who have strong, well-reasoned views and execute quickly, it is exceptional.

## The Interview Format

Linear runs a lean, focused interview process that mirrors its engineering culture — no waste, high signal.

**Initial conversation (30–45 min):** A call with an engineer or recruiter to understand your background, what you are looking for, and why Linear. Motivation matters significantly here — interviewers want to see that you use the product and understand what Linear is trying to accomplish.

**Take-home project (optional for some candidates):** Linear sometimes uses a take-home assignment to assess code quality and product thinking. If assigned, treat it like a production-quality piece of work — corner-cutting will be noticed.

**Technical interviews (2–3 rounds):**
- *Coding:* Real problems, not LeetCode trivia. Expect to write code that reflects your actual coding style. Performance, readability, and structure are all assessed.
- *System design:* Focus on real-time sync, collaborative state, and performance. Linear's sync engine is a core technical differentiator.
- *Product and culture:* How do you think about what to build? How do you handle competing priorities? How do you give and receive feedback?

## The Technical Bar

Linear's technical bar is genuinely high, and the focus areas reflect the product's core challenges.

**Real-time sync:** Linear uses a custom sync engine that keeps the client state consistent with the server efficiently and optimistically. Be prepared to discuss how you would design a system where local changes appear instantly, are synced to a server, and are reconciled when conflicts occur.

**Client performance:** Linear's frontend performance is a competitive advantage. Engineers need to understand rendering optimization, virtual DOM tradeoffs, and how to keep complex UIs fast at scale. React, TypeScript, and GraphQL are central to the stack.

**Data modeling:** Linear's domain (issues, cycles, projects, teams, relations) is more complex than it appears. Be ready to discuss schema design for hierarchical and relational data.

**TypeScript at scale:** Linear is an all-TypeScript shop. Strong typing, clean module architecture, and understanding of the TypeScript type system are expected.

Do not expect to survive on algorithmic problem-solving alone. Linear wants engineers who can build real software thoughtfully, not engineers who have memorized dynamic programming patterns.

## Why Linear Is Considered One of the Best Engineering Cultures in Tech

The praise Linear receives from engineers who work there and those who have interviewed there is unusually consistent. Several factors stand out:

**The product is the standard.** At Linear, the goal is to build software as good as what you use every day. That shared reference point creates a natural alignment between engineers and the company's mission.

**Small team, real ownership.** With a small headcount, there is no place to hide — but also no organizational friction between you and your work. Engineers ship things that matter and see the impact immediately.

**No process theater.** Linear is famous for shipping without bureaucracy. There are no layers of approval, no sprawling meetings, no roadmaps that exist only to justify headcount. The focus is on building.

**Exceptional caliber of colleagues.** Because Linear hires so selectively, the people you work with are uniformly excellent. The average quality of engineering judgment on the team is high.

For engineers who are tired of process-heavy organizations and want to work in an environment where craft, speed, and ownership are genuine values — not just words in a handbook — Linear is worth pursuing seriously.
