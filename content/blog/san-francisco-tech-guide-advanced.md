# San Francisco Tech Guide: The Complete Picture of Big Tech, Startups, and the Bay Area Market

San Francisco and the broader Bay Area remain the world's most concentrated technology job market. Despite much talk of tech's geographic dispersal post-pandemic, the Bay Area still accounts for a disproportionate share of senior engineering roles, high-value equity, and technical leadership opportunities. This guide gives a complete, honest picture of what working in SF means in 2026.

## The Bay Area Tech Landscape

**Big tech**: Google (Mountain View), Meta (Menlo Park), Apple (Cupertino), Salesforce (San Francisco), Slack/Salesforce (San Francisco), Twilio, Stripe, Airbnb, Lyft, DoorDash. All have significant Bay Area engineering organizations. Many have reduced their SF presence but remain anchored here.

**AI/ML companies**: OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Mistral (US HQ), Databricks, Scale AI — the current wave of AI companies is almost entirely Bay Area based. The proximity to academic talent (Berkeley, Stanford) and concentrated ML research community matters here in a way that's hard to replicate.

**Fintech**: Stripe (San Francisco, partially remote), Rippling, Brex, Ramp (NYC, but SF office). Square/Block (San Francisco).

**Startups**: YCombinator is in San Francisco. The Bay Area startup ecosystem remains unparalleled for density of Series A-C companies. These roles carry more risk, higher potential equity, and faster career velocity than big tech.

**Remote-first companies**: Many Bay Area companies are remote-first post-2020, but having an SF address on your resume still carries weight, and in-person networks still form here.

## FAANG Interview Preparation

The Bay Area big tech companies set the interview bar for the industry. Understanding what they test:

**Google**: Four coding rounds (medium-hard LeetCode, emphasis on efficient algorithms), two or more system design rounds, one "Googleyness and Leadership" behavioral round. Known for extremely high coding bar. Strong emphasis on code quality and clean solutions over just getting the answer.

**Meta**: Two coding rounds (medium-hard), one system design, one behavioral (product sense). Meta is known for being particularly focused on engineering velocity and practical decision-making. Their behavioral questions often include "Tell me about a project with unclear scope."

**Amazon**: Amazon's format uses Leadership Principles heavily. Every behavioral question explicitly maps to a principle. Coding is typically easier than Google/Meta but must be accompanied by strong behavioral answers. System design is important. STAR stories tied to Leadership Principles are essential preparation.

**Apple**: Technical expertise in your domain (iOS/macOS, ML, distributed systems) is weighted more heavily than at other FAANG. Expect deep technical questions about your specific area. Interview culture is more traditional and less process-driven than other FAANG.

**Stripe**: Known as one of the most rigorous technical interviewers in the industry. Heavy emphasis on code quality, API design, and systems thinking. The "Stripe Engineering blog" is essential reading to understand their standards.

## Compensation Reality (2026)

Bay Area tech compensation is the global high-water mark. Total compensation (base + equity + bonus) at senior levels:

**Mid-level engineer (L4/E4/SDE2)**:
- Base: $185,000 – $230,000
- Total comp: $250,000 – $380,000
- Equity varies: Google RSUs, Meta RSUs, Apple RSUs, startup options

**Senior engineer (L5/E5/SDE3)**:
- Base: $230,000 – $290,000
- Total comp: $380,000 – $550,000
- At Google L5, equity can be $200,000+/year in RSUs

**Staff engineer (L6/E6)**:
- Base: $290,000 – $370,000
- Total comp: $550,000 – $900,000+
- Staff at FAANG is genuinely life-changing compensation

**Levels.fyi** is the authoritative source for current FAANG compensation data by level and company.

**Cost of living reality**: A 1-bedroom apartment in San Francisco proper costs $3,200-4,500/month (2026). The Bay Area is significantly cheaper — Fremont, Oakland, South Bay cost $2,200-3,500. Housing dominates the cost differential. Tax rates are high: California 13.3% top marginal state income tax + federal.

Net of taxes and housing costs, a $400K total comp package in SF approximates a $250K total comp package in a low-tax, lower-housing-cost city like Austin or Miami. The equity upside and career velocity often still justify the Bay Area premium.

## Getting the Job

**Networking**: More important in the Bay Area than anywhere else. Referrals move applications from the resume black hole to an actual recruiter call. LinkedIn, local Meetups (SF JavaScript, Bay Area Python), and conferences are all relevant.

**Recruiting timeline**: FAANG processes run 6-12 weeks from application to offer. Startup processes run 2-4 weeks. Know your timeline and apply accordingly — don't get an offer before you're ready to compare it.

**Visa and relocation**: H-1B sponsorship is standard at FAANG and most large companies. Startups are hit-or-miss. The H-1B lottery has been increasingly competitive — plan for multi-year timelines if you're coming from outside the US. Many engineers enter on an OPT extension from US universities.

## The Realistic Picture

San Francisco is no longer the dominant force it was in 2018. Remote work has dispersed tech talent globally. Many companies have frozen real estate plans or moved HQs. But the concentration of capital, the network effects of the VC ecosystem, and the clustering of AI/ML companies ensure that the Bay Area remains uniquely impactful for certain career trajectories.

For engineers targeting FAANG roles, AI/ML companies, or senior technical leadership at high-growth startups, the Bay Area remains the densest market in the world. For engineers who prioritize quality of life, housing affordability, or specific domain expertise, many other cities now offer compelling alternatives.

The choice between the Bay Area and other markets is fundamentally about what you're optimizing for — and there's no single right answer.
