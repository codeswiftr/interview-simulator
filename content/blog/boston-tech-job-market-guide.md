# Boston Tech Job Market: Software Engineer Interview Guide

Boston's tech scene is powered by a unique combination of elite universities, biotech/life sciences companies, fintech firms, and consumer tech startups. With MIT and Harvard in its backyard, Boston produces a steady stream of deep-tech startups and has attracted major tech offices from companies worldwide. This guide helps software engineers navigate the Boston job market.

## The Boston Tech Ecosystem

Boston's tech landscape is more diverse than most markets:

**Consumer and growth tech**: HubSpot (marketing software, 2,000+ engineers), Wayfair (e-commerce, major engineering hub), DraftKings (sports betting, rapid growth), and Toast (restaurant tech, IPO'd) represent the high-growth consumer segment. These companies run competitive loops similar to Bay Area interviews with behavioral + technical rounds.

**Fintech and financial services**: Fidelity Investments, State Street, and Liberty Mutual all maintain large engineering organizations in Boston. These companies move slower but offer strong benefits and work-life balance. Interview culture tends to be more structured with longer hiring cycles.

**Biotech/life sciences tech**: Boston's biotech corridor (Kendall Square, the "most innovative square mile on earth") has spun out dozens of software-intensive companies. Moderna, Ginkgo Bioworks, and Broad Institute need software engineers who can work with scientific computing, genomics pipelines, and regulatory requirements.

**Defense and aerospace**: Raytheon, Draper Laboratory, and MIT Lincoln Laboratory hire software engineers with security clearances. These interviews often include systems programming, real-time constraints, and embedded systems.

**Deep tech and hardware**: Analog Devices, Boston Dynamics (now Hyundai), and numerous MIT spinouts operate in robotics, semiconductors, and hardware. Python for robotics control, ROS, and C++ embedded programming appear in these interviews.

## Salary Ranges and Compensation

Boston compensates competitively, though slightly below Bay Area and New York:

| Level | Base Salary | Total Comp |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| New grad (SWE I) | $110K–$135K | $130K–$160K |
| Mid-level (SWE II) | $140K–$175K | $165K–$220K |
| Senior (SWE III) | $175K–$220K | $210K–$280K |
| Staff | $220K–$280K | $280K–$370K |

Cost of living is lower than San Francisco (roughly 15-20% lower), making Boston compensation stretch further. Cambridge and Somerville are the preferred neighborhoods for engineers; commutes from the suburbs via commuter rail are common.

## Interview Culture and Process

**HubSpot**: Four-round process — recruiter screen, technical screen (data structures + product thinking), two technical loops (coding + system design), and a culture/values fit interview. HubSpot strongly emphasizes its HEART values (Humble, Empathetic, Adaptable, Remarkable, Transparent). Expect questions about scaling a B2B SaaS product.

**Wayfair**: Heavy algorithm interviews for engineering roles, plus system design for senior candidates. Known for fast processes (2-week close) and strong equity packages. Backend roles often involve distributed systems and recommendation algorithm discussions.

**DraftKings**: Competitive coding interviews similar to FAANG. Expect real-time system design questions (betting systems, odds calculation at scale) and behavioral rounds. Fast-paced culture — engineering team has grown 5x since 2020.

**HubSpot/fintech hybrids**: Many Boston fintech companies run 3-4 round processes with emphasis on system reliability and compliance considerations alongside technical depth.

## Where Engineers Work and Live

**Cambridge (Kendall Square)**: The epicenter of Boston's innovation — MIT, Google, Amazon, Biogen, and hundreds of startups cluster here. Short commute to downtown Boston via Red Line.

**Boston Seaport**: Growing tech corridor with Vertex Pharmaceuticals, PTC, and Amazon's HQ2 expansion. More expensive but newer office buildings.

**Downtown Boston/Financial District**: Fidelity, State Street, and traditional financial services firms. Suit culture is disappearing but still more formal than West Coast tech.

**Remote work**: Boston tech companies adapted well to remote work — many now offer hybrid (2-3 days in office) with strong remote cultures. This has expanded the talent pool and salary competition.

## Preparation Tips for Boston Interviews

1. Research the biotech/life sciences intersection if applying to Cambridge-area companies
2. Understand Boston's B2B SaaS culture — HubSpot and similar companies prize product thinking
3. Fintech companies ask compliance and data integrity questions — know ACID and audit trail patterns
4. Network through MIT Enterprise Forum, TechHub Boston, and Boston New Technology meetups
5. Boston companies often move faster than NY and slower than SF — expect 2-4 week cycles

Boston rewards engineers who combine technical depth with intellectual curiosity. The city's university culture permeates its tech companies — expect thoughtful discussion of tradeoffs rather than just algorithm tests.
