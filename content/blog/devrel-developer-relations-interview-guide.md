# Developer Relations Engineer Interview Guide: DevRel Career and Interview Prep

Developer Relations (DevRel) is one of tech's most misunderstood roles — and one of the most rewarding for engineers who love teaching, community, and building developer ecosystems. DevRel engineers sit at the intersection of software engineering, technical writing, product feedback, and community management. This guide covers what the role actually entails, what interviews test, and how to prepare.

## What Developer Relations Engineers Actually Do

DevRel encompasses several overlapping specializations:

**Developer Advocacy:**
The public face of a technical platform. Developer advocates create content (blog posts, tutorials, videos, conference talks), speak at events, and build sample applications that demonstrate the platform's capabilities. Think Kelsey Hightower (Kubernetes advocate at Google), Jessica Kerr (various DevRel roles), or Swyx (Temporal, formerly AWS). Advocates represent the company to developers externally.

**Developer Experience (DevEx/DX):**
Internal or external focus on making APIs, SDKs, documentation, and tooling easy to use. DevEx engineers file bugs, work with product teams, and sometimes write or review documentation. Heavily feedback-loop oriented — the job is making developer friction visible.

**Community Management:**
Building and nurturing technical communities (Discord servers, forums, GitHub Discussions, Stack Overflow tags). Community DevRel tracks community health metrics, moderates spaces, and creates programs (ambassador programs, hackathons, contributor programs).

**Technical Documentation:**
Some DevRel engineers own documentation — writing API references, tutorials, concept guides, and SDK samples. These roles blend engineering skill with technical communication.

## Technical Skills Tested in DevRel Interviews

DevRel interviews test different skills than pure engineering roles. Expect:

**Coding sample review:** You'll be asked to review poorly written code samples, documentation, or tutorials and improve them. This tests your ability to think like a developer encountering your platform for the first time.

**Live coding demonstration:** Write a working demo app using the company's API/SDK in real time, narrating your thought process. You're evaluated on: does the code work, is it idiomatic, would a developer find this example useful and understandable?

**Technical depth:** Deep knowledge of the platform's domain. A DevRel role at a Kubernetes company expects strong Kubernetes knowledge; a streaming data platform expects distributed systems literacy.

**Content creation assessment:** Write a blog post, create an outline for a tutorial, or give a mock conference talk on a technical topic. Strong DevRel candidates communicate complex topics accessibly without sacrificing accuracy.

**Empathy for developers:** You'll be asked about times you got frustrated with documentation, developer tools, or onboarding experiences. Authentic frustration with developer experience is a qualification.

## The Interview Process at Different Companies

**Large platform companies (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Twilio, Stripe):**
Structured process: recruiter screen → technical phone screen → content sample (take-home 1-2 hour exercise — often "write a tutorial for X using our API") → final rounds including: live coding demo, deep technical discussion with engineering team, DevRel strategy discussion with team lead, and sometimes a short presentation.

**Developer tool startups:**
More informal; often start with a casual conversation with the Head of DevRel or CTO. Expect to discuss your personal projects, blog posts, conference talks, or community contributions. Your online presence matters significantly.

**Content quality is a portfolio:** Before applying to DevRel roles, create content. Write technical blog posts, give local meetup talks, build sample projects with public GitHub repos, answer Stack Overflow questions. Your body of work is evaluated.

## What Separates Strong DevRel Candidates

**The empathy gap:** Strong DevRel engineers can switch between "expert mode" and "beginner mode" fluidly. They remember what it felt like to not know something and communicate to that person, not to peers who already know the platform.

**Product thinking:** The best DevRel engineers actively improve the product. They file actionable bug reports, propose API improvements based on developer feedback patterns, and close the loop between developer confusion and product team priorities.

**Community metrics fluency:** Know what metrics actually matter (developer activation rate, API adoption, community health scores, content engagement) vs vanity metrics (Twitter followers, event attendance). Be able to discuss how you'd measure the impact of a DevRel program.

**The "500 developers on a stage" test:** Can you give an engaging, technically accurate talk that resonates with an audience of engineers? Conference speaking skills — preparation, delivery, handling live Q&A — are often a formal part of the hiring assessment.

## Salary and Career Trajectory

DevRel salaries vary widely by company type:

**Typical ranges (2026):**
- Junior DevRel / Developer Advocate I: $100K–$140K
- Mid-level Developer Advocate: $140K–$180K
- Senior Developer Advocate: $180K–$240K
- Staff / Principal DevRel / Head of DevRel: $220K–$320K+

**Career paths from DevRel:**
- Product Manager (natural pivot — you deeply understand developer needs)
- Engineering Manager (for DevRel engineers who lean technical)
- Founder (many DevRel engineers start companies leveraging their ecosystem knowledge)
- Developer Experience Lead (focused on SDK/API design and documentation infrastructure)

DevRel is an excellent career path for engineers who want to have impact at scale — helping thousands of developers succeed — while staying connected to code, community, and the bleeding edge of technology.
