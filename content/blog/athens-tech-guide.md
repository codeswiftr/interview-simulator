# Athens Tech Hub: Interview Guide for Greece's Rising Startup Scene

Athens may not be the first city that comes to mind when you think of European tech, but it has undergone a quiet transformation over the past decade. Fueled by EU funding, a highly educated workforce, and a dramatically lower cost of living than Western European capitals, Athens has become one of the continent's most interesting emerging tech ecosystems — and a serious target for engineers looking to work in Europe without the London or Amsterdam price tag.

## The Athens Tech Ecosystem

The Greek capital's tech scene is concentrated around a handful of key districts. **Syntagma** and the nearby neighborhoods of **Kolonaki** and **Marousi** host corporate offices and larger tech employers. The newly redeveloped **Hellinikon** site — a former international airport — is being transformed into a €8 billion smart city and innovation hub that will eventually anchor a new wave of startups and R&D centers.

Key verticals:

- **Shipping and maritime tech**: Greece controls one of the world's largest merchant fleets. Companies like Wärtsilä Digital and a cluster of homegrown maritime SaaS providers are building vessel management, port logistics, and emissions tracking tools.
- **Tourism and travel tech**: Tourism accounts for ~25% of Greek GDP. Companies like TravelPerk (Spanish-Greek hybrid) and local OTAs are perpetually hiring engineers who understand high-throughput booking systems.
- **Fintech**: Viva Wallet (acquired by JP Morgan) put Athens on the fintech map globally. Others like Hellas Direct and Instashop (food delivery, acquired by Delivery Hero) followed.
- **AI and ML**: The National Technical University of Athens (NTUA) produces exceptional ML engineers. Several European AI companies have opened Athens R&D outposts specifically to tap this talent.
- **Cybersecurity**: Greek engineers have a disproportionate presence in European CTF competitions and infosec, and companies like Obrela Security Industries are building global products from Athens.

## Salary Benchmarks (2025–2026)

Salaries are in EUR. Greece's cost of living is roughly 40–50% lower than Germany or the Netherlands, making mid-level salaries highly competitive in lifestyle terms.

Remote roles for Northern/Western European or US companies can pay significantly above these ranges while benefiting from Athens's cost structure.

## EU Opportunities and Mobility

One of Athens's greatest assets for non-EU engineers is Greece's **Digital Nomad Visa** and its straightforward EU Golden Visa pathways. For EU citizens, Athens is accessible without any visa complexity. For engineers from outside the EU, Greece has streamlined several residency pathways tied to tech employment.

Working in Athens also means access to the full EU single market: GDPR compliance experience, EU AI Act familiarity, and understanding of pan-European data privacy are valuable differentiators.

## Interview Norms and Culture

**Warm but thorough.** Greek interview culture blends Mediterranean relationship-building with serious technical rigor, especially at EU-funded or internationally connected companies. Expect coffee, conversation about your background, and then a pivot to technical depth.

**Academic credentials carry weight.** Greek engineering culture has strong roots in academia. Mentioning relevant research, open-source contributions, or competitive programming backgrounds resonates strongly with interviewers.

**English is standard.** Nearly all tech interviews in Athens are conducted in English, even at Greek companies. This makes it highly accessible for international candidates.

**Take-home projects are common.** Mid-sized Athens startups typically give a 3–5 day take-home project rather than live coding. Expect a realistic mini-project rather than an abstract puzzle.

**Greek time is real, but tech interviews are punctual.** Show up on time for video or in-person interviews — this signals professionalism.

## Preparing for an Athens Interview

1. **Know EU regulation basics.** GDPR compliance, data residency requirements, and the EU AI Act are live topics — familiarity signals seniority.
2. **Understand Greek-specific market challenges.** Slow banking infrastructure, cash-heavy economy (changing fast), and high SME density shape what products need to do.
3. **Maritime and tourism tech.** If interviewing for these verticals, understand the specific data models: vessel AIS tracking, dynamic pricing in hospitality, multilingual UX.
4. **Prepare for architecture discussions.** Athens companies tend to be small but technically ambitious — senior roles almost always include a system design round.
5. **Show genuine interest in the ecosystem.** Referencing Viva Wallet's acquisition story, NTUA's ML output, or the Hellinikon smart-city project demonstrates you've done more than a Google search.

## Community and Networking

Athens has a growing tech community centered around **Athens Technology Center (ATC)**, **Egg (Enter-Grow-Go)** startup incubator, and events like **StartupGrind Athens** and **TechSalonika** (Thessaloniki-based but nationally networked). The **Greek Startup Association** (GRECA) and **Endeavor Greece** connect international engineers with the local ecosystem.

Athens is not a backup plan — it's a genuine strategic choice for engineers who want European scale, Mediterranean quality of life, and a tech scene on the rise.
