# Chicago Tech Job Market Guide

Chicago is the third-largest tech market in the United States, often underestimated because it lacks the concentration of a San Francisco or the cachet of New York. The city's engineering ecosystem is built on its economic strengths: finance and trading, enterprise software, logistics and supply chain, healthcare, and a growing startup community anchored in the Fulton Market district. For engineers who want major-city scale without Bay Area costs or New York intensity, Chicago offers a compelling combination.

## Chicago's Tech Ecosystem

**Quantitative finance and trading**: Chicago's most distinctive tech sector. The city is the birthplace of options trading (the CBOE) and algorithmic trading (CME Group). Major trading firms with large Chicago engineering teams: Citadel and Citadel Securities (one of the largest market makers in the world), Jump Trading, DRW, IMC, Optiver, and Trading Technologies. These firms hire hundreds of engineers annually for systems programming, quantitative development, and risk infrastructure. Compensation is at the upper end of the industry.

**Enterprise software**: Salesforce has a substantial Chicago presence (partly through the Mulesoft acquisition and organic growth). Morningstar (investment research and data), Groupon (now significantly smaller), Uptake (industrial AI), and numerous B2B SaaS companies make Chicago a strong enterprise software market. The city's proximity to major corporations across the Midwest makes it a natural enterprise sales base, which drives demand for enterprise-facing engineering.

**Logistics and supply chain**: Chicago's geography — railroad hub, O'Hare airport, access to the Great Lakes — made it the nation's logistics center. Companies like Echo Global Logistics, Coyote Logistics (acquired by UPS), and numerous supply chain tech startups have engineering teams here. The intersection of logistics operations and software is a distinctive Chicago niche.

**Healthcare tech**: Chicago is home to major health systems (Northwestern Medicine, Rush, UChicago Medicine) with active digital health initiatives, and healthcare IT companies including Outcome Health, Nuvance Health, and insurance tech companies.

**Startup ecosystem**: The Fulton Market district (Chicago's fastest-growing tech neighborhood) and the Loop host growing concentrations of tech companies. Notable Chicago startups: Grubhub (founded in Chicago, now Just Eat Takeaway), Orbitz (founding city), Braintree (acquired by PayPal), Folio Investments, and a steady stream of B2B and fintech companies.

## Salary Ranges

Chicago salaries are lower than San Francisco and New York in base terms, but cost of living is significantly lower than either:

**Junior Software Engineer (0-3 years)**: $90,000–$125,000 base
**Mid-level Software Engineer (3-6 years)**: $125,000–$165,000 base
**Senior Software Engineer (6+ years)**: $160,000–$220,000 base
**Staff/Principal Engineer**: $210,000–$290,000 total compensation
**Trading/Quant Engineer (finance)**: $200,000–$450,000+ TC — comparable to New York finance roles

**Illinois income tax**: Illinois has a flat income tax rate of 4.95%, lower than California (13.3% top) but higher than Texas or Washington (no state income tax). Net take-home is meaningfully better than California for high earners.

**Housing**: Chicago has the most affordable housing of any major US tech market. A 2-bedroom apartment in desirable Chicago neighborhoods (Lincoln Park, River North, West Loop) runs $2,500-$4,000/month versus $4,000-$7,000+ in San Francisco or New York. Homeownership is realistic for dual-income tech couples in a way it isn't in coastal cities.

## The Chicago Engineering Culture

**Midwestern professional culture**: Chicago's engineering culture reflects broader Midwestern norms — directness without East Coast intensity, less visible status signaling than San Francisco, strong work ethic with clear work-life separation. Engineers who find Bay Area culture performative or New York culture aggressive often find Chicago a comfortable fit.

**Office presence**: Chicago tech companies generally expect more in-office time than remote-first companies. Financial firms are often 4-5 days. Tech companies typically 2-3 days hybrid. The Chicago downtown core (Loop, Fulton Market) makes commuting manageable compared to other major markets.

**Strong state university pipeline**: University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC) is one of the top computer science programs in the country. Northwestern's McCormick School of Engineering, UChicago's CS department, and Illinois Tech produce consistent engineering talent that fills Chicago companies. UIUC alumni networks are particularly strong in the city.

## Getting Into Chicago Tech

**For trading firm roles**: C++ and systems programming experience are highly valued. Algorithmic thinking, performance optimization, and low-latency systems knowledge differentiate candidates at firms like Citadel and Jump. The compensation premium at these firms is real but the bar is correspondingly high.

**Networking**: Chicago's tech community is active — Chicago Tech Slack workspace, Built In Chicago as a resource, and meetups around specific stacks (Chicago Cloud Computing, Chicago Python, Chicago JS). Less frenetic than Bay Area networking but genuine connections matter.

**Visa sponsorship**: Major tech companies and financial firms in Chicago actively sponsor H-1B visas. The financial sector is particularly accustomed to international hiring.

Chicago rewards engineers who value financial stability, quality of life, and career growth without the extremes of either coast. The city's combination of trading firm compensation at the high end, solid tech company mid-market, and genuinely livable costs makes it a compelling long-term choice for engineers who want to build both career and life simultaneously.
