# Interview Prep Timeline: 4-Week, 8-Week, and 12-Week Plans

The most common question from engineers starting interview prep: "How much time do I need, and what do I study when?" The honest answer depends on your current level and target companies. This guide provides three structured timelines.

## Prerequisites Before Any Timeline

Before starting, assess your baseline:
- Solve 5 LeetCode mediums. If you can solve 3+ without hints, you need less DSA time.
- Design a system you haven't designed before (e.g., design Uber). If you can get 70% of the components right, system design needs refinement, not foundational work.
- Recall 3 STAR behavioral stories without preparing them first. If you can, behavioral prep will be lighter.

This baseline sets your starting point for each timeline.

## 4-Week Plan (Urgent/Opportunity)

Use when you have an unexpected opportunity or are mid-search with interviews imminent.

**Week 1: DSA Foundations**
- Days 1-2: Arrays, strings, two pointers, sliding window (20 easy/medium problems)
- Days 3-4: Hash maps, sets, frequency counting patterns (15 problems)
- Days 5-6: Binary search variations (10 problems)
- Day 7: Mock coding interview + review weak areas

**Week 2: Core Algorithms**
- Days 1-2: Trees (traversals, BFS/DFS, LCA) — 20 problems
- Days 3-4: Dynamic programming basics (Fibonacci, climbing stairs, knapsack) — 15 problems
- Days 5-6: Graphs (BFS, DFS, topological sort) — 15 problems
- Day 7: Mock coding interview

**Week 3: System Design + Behavioral**
- Days 1-2: System design foundations (read System Design Interview Vol 1, chapters 1-5)
- Day 3: Design a URL shortener (full walkthrough)
- Day 4: Design a chat system
- Day 5: Design a social feed / news feed
- Day 6-7: Prepare 6 behavioral stories (STAR format), practice aloud

**Week 4: Integration and Mocks**
- Days 1-2: Fill gaps from weeks 1-3, review most common patterns
- Days 3-5: One full mock interview daily (coding + system design)
- Days 6-7: Review performance in mocks, targeted practice, mental prep

Expected outcome: prepared for mid-tier companies (well-known tech companies, growth startups). May struggle with top-tier FAANG in 4 weeks starting from zero.

## 8-Week Plan (Standard)

Use when you have 2 months before target interviews. Most engineers can get FAANG-ready in 8 weeks with consistent daily practice.

**Weeks 1-2: DSA Fundamentals**
- Arrays, strings, hashing, two pointers, sliding window
- Target: 40 problems/week, ~6 problems/day
- Focus on understanding patterns, not memorizing solutions

**Weeks 3-4: Trees, Graphs, DP Basics**
- Binary trees all traversals, BST operations
- Graphs: BFS, DFS, connected components, shortest paths (Dijkstra, BFS for unweighted)
- DP: memoization pattern, tabulation, classic problems (coin change, LCS, edit distance)
- Target: 35 problems/week

**Weeks 5-6: Advanced Topics + System Design Start**
- Advanced DP (interval DP, tree DP, state machine DP)
- Heaps, priority queues, top-K patterns
- Union-Find
- System design: read System Design Interview Vol 1 fully
- Design 3 systems in depth: URL shortener, rate limiter, key-value store

**Weeks 7-8: Integration, Mocks, Behavioral**
- Daily LeetCode (maintain sharpness)
- 3 system design mocks total
- 2 coding mocks per week
- Behavioral: 8 stories prepared and rehearsed
- Research target companies: common interview questions, engineering blog posts

Expected outcome: prepared for FAANG and equivalent. 70%+ pass rate for candidates who followed the plan consistently.

## 12-Week Plan (Deep Preparation)

Use when you're targeting top-tier companies (Google L5+, Meta E5+, staff-level anywhere) or are coming from a non-algorithmic background.

**Weeks 1-3: DSA Fundamentals (Deep)**
- All foundational patterns with emphasis on understanding why, not just what
- Solve each problem type 3 different ways before moving on
- 250 problems total over 3 weeks

**Weeks 4-6: Advanced Algorithms**
- All DP patterns (50 DP problems minimum)
- Advanced graph algorithms (Bellman-Ford, Floyd-Warshall, minimum spanning tree)
- Segment trees, BITs (Fenwick trees) for range query problems
- Backtracking patterns (N-Queens, word search, combination sum)

**Weeks 7-8: System Design (Deep)**
- Read: System Design Interview Vol 1+2, DDIA (chapters relevant to distributed systems)
- Design 8+ systems with full written solutions
- Study real architecture blog posts (Airbnb, Netflix, Uber engineering blogs)
- Practice system design aloud — timing yourself

**Weeks 9-10: Behavioral and Company Research**
- Develop 10+ STAR stories covering all competencies (leadership, conflict, failure, ambiguity)
- Research each target company: tech stack, products, recent launches, engineering culture
- Practice behavioral responses until they feel natural, not rehearsed

**Weeks 11-12: Integration and Mocks**
- Full mock interviews 3x/week (2 coding, 1 system design)
- Daily LeetCode (2-3 problems to maintain speed)
- Identify remaining gaps from mock feedback, targeted remediation
- Interview simulation: simulate actual day ( 4 rounds back-to-back)

Expected outcome: optimal preparation for the most competitive roles.

## Daily Practice Rhythm

Regardless of timeline, consistency beats intensity. Daily 2-hour sessions outperform weekly 14-hour marathons.

**Optimal daily structure:**
- 15 min: Warm-up (1 easy problem you've seen before)
- 45-60 min: Main focus (new problems or designated topic)
- 30 min: Review/study (read editorial for any problem you needed hints on)
- 15 min: Spaced repetition (re-solve 1 problem from 2 weeks ago from scratch)

Never end a practice session without understanding why your solution worked. Implementation without understanding doesn't help in interviews.

## The Week Before Interviews

Don't try to learn new material the week before. Instead: review your weak areas, do 1-2 easy/medium problems per day to stay sharp, review your behavioral stories, research the specific company and interviewers. Rest, sleep well, reduce caffeine.
