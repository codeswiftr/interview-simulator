# Accra Tech Scene Guide: Software Engineering Jobs in Ghana

Accra has emerged as one of West Africa's most promising tech hubs, distinct from Lagos in character but growing fast in its own right. Ghana's political stability, English-speaking professional culture, and strategic position as a regional gateway have attracted international investment and a growing density of tech companies. For software engineers, Accra offers a combination of local opportunity and a springboard to international remote careers.

## Why Accra?

Ghana has consistently ranked among Africa's more stable democracies, and Accra reflects that stability in its business environment. The city hosts the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) secretariat, signaling its ambitions as a pan-African hub. For tech companies building for African markets, Accra provides access to the broader ECOWAS economic zone.

**Accra Tech Hub (formerly mPedigree's network hub)** and **MEST Africa** (Meltwater Entrepreneurial School of Technology) have been critical institutions in building the local ecosystem. MEST in particular has produced dozens of successful Ghanaian startups and maintains a pan-African presence.

## The Ecosystem

**Fintech:** Similar to Nigeria, Ghana's fintech sector is driven by mobile money penetration — MTN Mobile Money (MoMo) has over 18 million users in a country of 32 million. Companies building on top of this infrastructure, like Zeepay, Hubtel, and ExpressPay, need engineers who understand mobile money APIs, USSD, and agent banking networks.

**Agriculture tech:** Ghana's agricultural sector is significant, and companies like Farmerline and AgroCenta are building data platforms and market linkage tools for smallholder farmers. This requires creative engineering under connectivity and literacy constraints.

**E-commerce and logistics:** Jumia Ghana, Tonaton (classifieds), and a growing cluster of delivery companies are building for Accra's growing middle class. Last-mile logistics in a city without standardized addressing is a genuine engineering challenge.

**International companies with Accra offices:** Google has an Africa team with presence in Accra. Andela places engineers from Ghana with global remote employers. Microsoft and various European development finance institutions fund tech programs that create employment.

**Remote work:** A significant and growing segment of Accra engineers work remotely for international companies, similar to the pattern seen in Lagos.

## Salary Benchmarks

Local Ghanaian tech salaries (in GHS) have grown but remain modest by international standards:

- Junior engineers: 3,000–6,000 GHS/month
- Mid-level engineers: 6,000–12,000 GHS/month
- Senior engineers: 12,000–20,000 GHS/month

For engineers working remotely for international employers:
- Mid-level: $2,000–$4,500/month (USD)
- Senior: $4,500–$9,000+/month (USD)

The gap between local and international compensation is substantial, which drives significant interest in remote work and international career development among Accra's engineers.

## Interview Culture

### At Local Ghanaian Companies

**Pragmatic and relationship-oriented.** Ghanaian business culture places value on relationship-building and trust. First interviews at local companies often have a significant rapport-building component before technical questions begin. Don't mistake this for lacking rigor — technical assessment follows, often through take-home projects or pair programming sessions.

**Problem-solving over puzzle-solving.** Few Accra startups use LeetCode-style algorithmic interviews. Practical assessments — "here's a data model, how would you improve it?" or "walk us through how you'd build a payment integration" — are more common.

**Communication and client skills are valued.** Many Ghanaian tech companies work with international clients or serve diverse user populations. The ability to communicate technical concepts clearly and write professional documentation is explicitly evaluated.

### At International Companies or for Remote Roles

Expect standard international interview processes. If you're targeting a Google Africa role, a remote position with a US startup, or placement through Andela, the interview process will be identical to international standards: algorithmic coding rounds, system design, behavioral interviews.

This is where deliberate preparation pays off significantly.

## In-Demand Technical Skills

- **JavaScript/TypeScript and Node.js** — the default stack at most Accra startups
- **Python** — data engineering, ML, and backend APIs
- **React and React Native** — frontend and mobile development
- **Mobile money API integration** — MTN MoMo, Vodafone Cash, AirtelTigo Money
- **SQL and database design** — PostgreSQL is widely used
- **Cloud platforms** — AWS experience is particularly valued for senior roles

## MEST Africa and the Training Pipeline

MEST (Meltwater Entrepreneurial School of Technology) deserves special attention. Its intensive two-year program trains software engineers, product managers, and business people together, producing startup founders and senior engineers who are comfortable across the full product development stack. MEST alumni companies have raised over $100M combined and operate across Africa and internationally.

If you're preparing to work with or interview at MEST portfolio companies, expect technically sophisticated interviewers who also understand the business context of engineering decisions.

## Community and Events

GhanaJS, DevFest Accra, and the Ghana Developer Community are active. Accra Tech Week has grown into a regional event attracting speakers and attendees from across West Africa. These events are genuine networking opportunities — many Accra tech jobs are filled through community connections.

## Preparing for Accra Interviews

**Research the mobile money landscape deeply.** If you're interviewing at any fintech company, understand MTN MoMo's API architecture, the Bank of Ghana's mobile money regulations, and how interoperability between networks works. This domain knowledge is rare and valued.

**Build a visible portfolio.** GitHub activity, open source contributions, and personal projects carry significant weight with Accra hiring managers. The signal of "writes code outside of work" is taken seriously.

**Target the remote work pipeline systematically.** If your goal is international remote work, treat interview preparation as a structured project: work through algorithm problems consistently, practice system design with a study partner or tool, and build your communication skills deliberately. Interview Simulator's AI-powered practice sessions can help you get comfortable with international-standard technical interviews before the real thing.

**Understand the AfCFTA opportunity.** For engineers interested in pan-African product roles, understanding the African Continental Free Trade Area and what it means for digital commerce and payments is a meaningful differentiator in interviews at companies building for the African market.

Accra's tech scene is at an inflection point. The infrastructure, talent pipeline, and international attention are all converging. Engineers who invest in both local market knowledge and international-standard technical skills will be well-positioned to ride this wave.
