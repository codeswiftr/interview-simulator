# Prague Tech Market Guide: Software Engineering Jobs and Startups in Czech Republic

Prague has evolved from a post-communist outsourcing destination into a genuine tech ecosystem with product companies, global R&D centers, and a thriving startup scene. For software engineers considering opportunities in Central Europe, Prague offers a compelling combination: strong salaries relative to local costs, a high quality of life, and an increasingly sophisticated technical community.

## The Tech Ecosystem

Prague's tech market is diverse and growing. Several distinct clusters define it:

**Global Technology Centers:** Avast (now part of Gen Digital), Oracle, Red Hat, and Kiwi.com all have significant engineering presence in Prague. These aren't call centers — they're doing core product development, often with significant autonomy from their US or global headquarters.

**Czech-Born Success Stories:** Kiwi.com (travel tech, bootstrapped to unicorn), Rohlik Group (online grocery), and Mall.cz (e-commerce) represent locally-grown scale-ups that are increasingly competing for senior engineering talent internationally.

**Gaming and Creative Tech:** The Czech Republic has a strong gaming tradition. Warhorse Studios (Kingdom Come: Deliverance) and several smaller studios are based in Prague, and the broader game tech ecosystem creates crossover interest in graphics, simulation, and real-time systems.

**Fintech:** Twisto (BNPL), Creditas, and a growing cluster of open banking startups are building on EU regulatory frameworks. PSD2 compliance expertise is valued here.

**Remote-First Companies:** Like Warsaw, Prague attracts engineers working remotely for Western European and US companies, benefiting from the favorable exchange rate and quality of life.

## Salary Benchmarks

Czech salaries for software engineers have risen sharply as the local market has matured:

- Junior: 60,000–90,000 CZK/month gross
- Mid-level: 90,000–140,000 CZK/month gross
- Senior: 140,000–200,000 CZK/month gross
- Staff/Principal: 200,000–280,000 CZK/month gross

At global tech companies like Oracle or Red Hat's Prague office, total compensation (including bonuses and equity) can be substantially higher and approach Western European levels. Many experienced engineers also work as contractors, billing in EUR or USD.

## Interview Culture

Czech interview culture combines Central European pragmatism with an increasingly international approach as companies compete for global talent.

### What to Expect

**Technical depth is respected.** Czech engineers, as a cultural norm, tend to be methodical and precise. Interviewers appreciate candidates who think out loud, acknowledge uncertainty, and demonstrate rigorous thinking — not just confident bluffing.

**Take-home projects are standard.** At product companies like Kiwi.com or Rohlik, expect a realistic take-home assignment: perhaps designing a flight search API, a pricing engine, or an inventory management service. These are evaluated on code quality, testing discipline, and documentation — not just whether the solution works.

**System design rounds are rigorous at scale-ups.** Companies that have experienced rapid growth have lived through scaling pain. Questions about database replication, service mesh, and observability aren't theoretical — they reflect real problems these teams have solved.

**Behavioral interviews are becoming more structured.** Influenced by American interview practices, larger Czech companies now include structured behavioral rounds (STAR format). Prepare stories about conflict resolution, technical leadership, and handling ambiguity.

### Language

Nearly all technical interviews at international companies or scale-ups are conducted in English. At smaller Czech companies or traditional IT firms, interviews may be in Czech. Confirm the language upfront.

## Key Technical Skills in Demand

- **Java and Kotlin** are dominant in enterprise and fintech
- **Python** is strong in data, ML, and backend API work
- **TypeScript/React** for frontend across most product companies
- **Scala** still has a presence in data engineering (Kiwi.com has historically used it)
- **Kubernetes and cloud infrastructure** (AWS/GCP) — strong demand at scale-ups
- **Go** — growing in platform engineering roles

## The Community

Prague's tech community is active. Meetup groups for Python, Java, DevOps, and machine learning run regularly. PyCon CZ is well-attended, and the local Stack Overflow developer survey consistently shows Prague engineers as among the most engaged in Europe.

Czech Technical University (CVUT) in Prague produces strong engineering graduates, and Charles University contributes computer science talent. Both have active alumni networks in the industry.

## Practical Advice for Job Seekers

**Research the company's actual engineering challenges.** Prague companies are proud of the technical problems they solve — Kiwi.com's flight combination algorithms, Rohlik's same-day delivery logistics, Oracle's database internals. Showing genuine curiosity about these challenges distinguishes you immediately.

**Understand EU work authorization.** Czech Republic is in the EU (Schengen area). EU/EEA citizens have free movement. Non-EU engineers should research the Employee Card (Zaměstnanecká karta) process — many Prague tech companies sponsor it for experienced engineers.

**Know the local startup funding landscape.** Czech startups are increasingly funded by EU structural funds, Czech venture capital (Rockaway Capital, Kaya VC), and pan-European VCs. Demonstrating awareness of how this affects product velocity and technical decision-making shows business maturity.

**Visit during interview trips.** If you're relocating, visiting Prague for an on-site interview is worth it. The city's livability often closes deals — it's consistently ranked among Europe's most beautiful and affordable capitals.
