# Warsaw Tech Job Market: Poland's Largest Tech Hub, Salaries, and EU Work Authorization

Warsaw has emerged as one of Central and Eastern Europe's most significant technology hubs, attracting global companies establishing European engineering centers, spawning homegrown unicorns, and retaining engineering talent that would previously have emigrated to London or Berlin. The city offers a compelling combination: competitive compensation, EU regulatory environment, rapidly improving quality of life, and a deep pool of technically strong engineers. For international tech professionals, Warsaw is an increasingly attractive alternative to Western European capitals at a significantly lower cost of living.

## The Market Structure: Outsourcing vs. Product Companies

Understanding Warsaw's market requires understanding a structural distinction that shapes careers and compensation differently: outsourcing/IT services companies vs. product companies.

**Outsourcing and IT services** (historically dominant):
- **Infosys, Cognizant, Capgemini, Accenture Technology**: All have large Warsaw delivery centers
- **EPAM Systems**: The largest IT services company operating in Poland; significant Warsaw presence
- **Luxoft** (DXC subsidiary): Financial services focused, Warsaw engineering hub
- **Stefanini**: Polish-origin services company, global operations

These companies offer stable employment, exposure to diverse client technologies, and structured career paths. Compensation is lower than product companies and Western benchmarks; the work is more execution-focused than product development.

**Product companies** (growing, better-compensated):
- **Allegro** — Poland's largest e-commerce platform, Polish-founded, Warsaw-headquartered; considered the gold standard for Polish tech employment
- **Bolt** — Estonian-founded mobility platform, significant Warsaw engineering presence
- **Brainly** — EdTech, Polish-founded, Kraków-headquartered but Warsaw presence
- **DocPlanner** — Polish-founded healthtech, Warsaw headquarters, $800M+ valuation
- **Booksy** — appointment booking, Polish-founded, Warsaw-based

**Global tech companies' Warsaw engineering centers**:
- **Google** — significant Warsaw engineering office (one of the largest outside the US)
- **Amazon** — AWS and retail tech engineering in Warsaw
- **Samsung R&D** — one of Samsung's largest R&D centers outside Korea
- **Mobidev, Netguru, Boldare**: Polish product development companies that blend services and product work

## Salary Benchmarks

Polish salaries are typically quoted in PLN (Polish Zloty) or EUR. The EUR/PLN exchange rate fluctuates but has been approximately 4.2–4.3 PLN per EUR in recent years. Salaries in Warsaw are the highest in Poland, roughly 20–30% above Kraków and Wrocław.

**Monthly gross salaries (B2B contract — common for senior engineers):**

| Level | IT Services | Polish Product Companies | Global Tech (Google, Amazon) |
|-------|-------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| Junior (0–2 yrs) | 8,000–12,000 PLN | 10,000–16,000 PLN | 14,000–20,000 PLN |
| Mid-level (2–5 yrs) | 14,000–20,000 PLN | 18,000–28,000 PLN | 25,000–38,000 PLN |
| Senior (5–8 yrs) | 20,000–28,000 PLN | 28,000–42,000 PLN | 38,000–55,000 PLN |
| Staff/Lead | 28,000–38,000 PLN | 40,000–60,000 PLN | 50,000–75,000 PLN |

**EUR equivalents for Senior level**: ~€6,700–€10,000/month (B2B gross), or approximately €80,000–€120,000 annually.

**B2B vs. employment contract (UoP)**: Most senior Polish engineers work on B2B contracts (sole trader invoicing the company) rather than employment contracts. B2B offers lower tax rates for higher earners but requires setting up a sole proprietorship and managing ZUS (social insurance) contributions independently. Employment contracts include employer-paid benefits and are simpler administratively.

## Warsaw-Specific Context

**Cost of living**: Warsaw is substantially cheaper than Western European capitals. Monthly living expenses for a single engineer in Warsaw: ~6,000–8,000 PLN (€1,400–1,900), including rent for a 1-bedroom apartment in the city center. This means a senior engineer earning 40,000 PLN/month has significant disposable income relative to peers in London, Amsterdam, or Stockholm.

**Housing**: Warsaw's rental market has tightened significantly since 2022 due to population growth and Ukrainian refugee inflow. A 1-bedroom apartment in Śródmieście (city center), Mokotów, or Żoliborz runs 3,500–5,500 PLN/month. Buying has become expensive by Polish historical standards but remains affordable relative to Western Europe.

**Tech neighborhoods**:
- **Mokotów (Służewiec)**: Warsaw's historical tech district, dense with outsourcing company offices
- **Wola / Daszyńskiego**: Newer business district, home to Google's Warsaw office and modern product company headquarters
- **Śródmieście**: City center, startups and smaller product companies
- **Praga (Praga Południe)**: Up-and-coming district with creative/startup energy

## The Polish Tech Talent Pool

Poland has one of Europe's strongest engineering talent pipelines. Key universities feeding Warsaw's tech market:

- **Warsaw University of Technology (Politechnika Warszawska)**: Consistently ranked in top 5 European technical universities for engineering output; CS graduates are in high demand globally
- **University of Warsaw**: Strong computer science and mathematics program
- **SGH Warsaw School of Economics**: Business-tech intersection, product management and engineering management talent

Polish engineers are known in the global market for strong fundamentals, mathematical rigor, and competitive programming culture (Poland produces disproportionate numbers of ACM/ICPC competitors). The talent is deep at the algorithmic and systems level; product and UX culture has historically been weaker but is improving rapidly.

## EU Work Authorization

**EU citizens**: Full freedom of movement. No visa required; can work in Poland immediately upon arrival.

**Non-EU citizens** (the most common scenarios):

**EU Blue Card**: For highly qualified non-EU workers. Requires a job offer above the salary threshold (currently ~6,700 PLN/month gross — well below market for senior engineers), valid degree, and employment contract. Valid for 3 years, path to Polish PR after 5 years of legal residence in the EU.

**Work permit (Zezwolenie na pracę) + Temporary Residence (Karta Pobytu)**: Standard employer-sponsored path. Employer applies for work permit; you apply for temporary residence permit. Processing: 2–4 months. Valid for up to 3 years, renewable.

**Polish Card (Karta Polaka)**: For citizens of former Soviet states who have Polish heritage. Provides simplified access to work authorization and residency.

**Ukrainian citizens**: Since 2022, Ukrainian citizens have special temporary protection status in Poland, with full rights to work and access social services. This has significantly increased the Ukrainian engineering presence in Warsaw.

**Path to permanent residency**: After 5 years of continuous legal residence, non-EU citizens can apply for permanent EU long-term resident status, which provides freedom of movement throughout the EU/EEA.

## Career Trajectory in Warsaw

Engineers early in their career often use Warsaw's IT services sector as a training ground, building experience across diverse technology stacks and client domains. Moving to product companies after 3–4 years is a well-worn path that offers a compensation jump of 30–50% and more interesting technical challenges.

For senior engineers and engineering leaders, Warsaw's product company ecosystem — Allegro in particular — offers genuinely challenging distributed systems work at scale. Allegro's engineering blog is one of the best technical resources in the CEE region and reflects the quality of problems the team is solving.

For international engineers, Warsaw combines European stability, EU work authorization pathways, and a cost of living that allows meaningful savings — a rare combination in today's tech market.
