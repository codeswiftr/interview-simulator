# Looking for a Pramp Alternative? Here's What to Consider

Pramp built something genuinely useful: free, peer-to-peer technical interview practice with real-time collaboration. For a certain kind of candidate — motivated, well-networked, available on short notice — it works well. But a lot of people who start with Pramp eventually find themselves looking for something else. This post is for them.

We're not going to trash Pramp. The peer model has real advantages. What we'll do instead is help you figure out which approach fits your actual preparation situation.

## What Makes Pramp Worth Using

The core appeal of Pramp is human contact. You're practicing with another engineer who has skin in the game — they're prepping too, which means most sessions have a baseline level of seriousness. Peer interviews also give you something AI can't perfectly replicate: the experience of being observed and evaluated by a human while you think.

There's also a networking dimension. Some candidates have made genuine professional connections through Pramp sessions. And the price is hard to beat.

If you're a recent grad with a flexible schedule, a high tolerance for session rescheduling, and you benefit from the social accountability of committing to a specific time with a real person — Pramp is worth your time.

## Where Peer-Based Practice Falls Short

Here's where it gets honest.

**Scheduling friction is real.** Most working engineers don't have 90-minute windows that align with another engineer's availability on 24 hours' notice. When you have to schedule 3-5 days out, the cadence of practice breaks down. Consistency matters more than any single session.

**Skill level mismatch is common.** Pramp pairs you based on general experience level, but "mid-level engineer" covers an enormous range. You might get paired with someone who last interviewed five years ago, or someone who's done 40 Pramp sessions and knows every common problem cold. Neither scenario gives you calibrated feedback on your actual weak spots.

**Feedback quality is inconsistent.** When your peer gives you feedback, they're doing their best — but they're not a trained interviewer. They may miss things. They may be too polite. They may not know the specific rubric that FAANG companies use. The feedback you get is a function of who you happened to match with that day.

**The social dynamic can dilute honesty.** It's awkward to tell someone their answer was structurally incoherent when you'll be switching roles in 30 minutes. Most peers pull their punches. That's human. But it means you may leave sessions thinking you did better than you did.

## What AI Interview Practice Offers Instead

AI-based practice flips most of these constraints.

There's no scheduling. You practice when you have 20 minutes — at 6am before work, during lunch, after the kids are in bed. The consistency that's nearly impossible to maintain with peer scheduling becomes trivial.

Feedback is immediate, structured, and consistent. The same rubric applies to every session. You don't get "you did fine" — you get a breakdown of where your answer demonstrated the STAR structure and where it didn't, what technical details you communicated clearly, and what you skipped.

There's no social awkwardness. The AI will tell you your answer was weak in exactly the same tone it tells you it was strong. That's not a limitation — for most candidates, honest feedback is exactly what they're not getting.

## Who Benefits Most From Each Approach

**AI practice is better for you if:**
- You're employed full-time and can't reliably schedule 90-minute blocks
- You want consistent, rubric-based feedback on every session
- You're early in your prep and need to build fluency before facing a real human
- You want to practice at odd hours or in short bursts
- You've been burned before by inconsistent peer feedback

**Peer practice is better for you if:**
- You're close to interview-ready and want genuine human unpredictability
- You benefit strongly from social accountability and scheduled commitments
- You want to practice the specific dynamic of being watched and evaluated live
- You're trying to build a professional network alongside your prep

For most busy professionals, these aren't mutually exclusive. AI practice builds the baseline — fluency, structure, speed — so that when you do sit down with a peer or a real interviewer, you're not wasting their time on fundamentals.
