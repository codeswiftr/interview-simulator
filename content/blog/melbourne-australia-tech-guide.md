# Melbourne Tech Scene Guide: Software Engineering Jobs and Interview Culture in Australia

Melbourne is Australia's second-largest city and its tech capital for financial services, media, and enterprise software. While Sydney has more startup activity, Melbourne has a strong engineering culture, competitive salaries, excellent quality of life, and a growing fintech and deeptech ecosystem. If you're considering relocating to Australia or are already here, this guide covers what you need to know.

## The Melbourne Tech Landscape

Melbourne's tech industry is anchored by large enterprise and financial services companies: ANZ, NAB, Coles Group, Seek, REA Group (realestate.com.au), and Xero's Australian engineering operations. These provide stable, well-paying engineering roles with good work-life balance compared to startup culture.

The startup ecosystem has grown significantly — with companies like Canva (dual HQ in Sydney/Melbourne), Airwallex (founded in Melbourne), SafetyCulture, and Culture Amp calling Melbourne or Victoria home. The Fishburners and Stone & Chalk coworking communities serve early-stage startups.

**Top employers by category:**
- **Fintech:** Airwallex, CoinJar, Monoova, ANZ Worldline
- **Enterprise/media:** REA Group, Seek, AFL, Nine Entertainment
- **Health tech:** Telstra Health, Medibank, Healthscope
- **Retail/commerce:** Coles Digital, Officeworks tech, Catch
- **Consulting:** Thoughtworks (strong Melbourne presence), DXC, Accenture

## Salaries

Software engineering salaries in Melbourne (in AUD, 2025 figures):

| Level | Salary Range |
|-------|-------------|
| Junior (0-2 years) | $70K-$95K |
| Mid-level (2-5 years) | $95K-$130K |
| Senior (5+ years) | $130K-$170K |
| Staff/Principal | $170K-$220K |
| Engineering Manager | $150K-$200K |

Salaries at top-tier companies (Canva, Airwallex, senior FAANG-adjacent roles) can exceed these ranges. Cost of living is high in Melbourne — median house prices exceed AUD $900K — though renting is more manageable than Sydney. The AUD to USD conversion reduces purchasing power parity for those comparing to US salaries.

## Interview Culture

Australian tech interviews are generally more relaxed than US tech interviews. The culture values: directness, practical problem-solving over algorithmic puzzles, and cultural fit. Some differences from US interviewing:

**Less LeetCode-heavy:** Many Melbourne companies use practical take-home assignments or architecture discussions rather than whiteboard algorithm questions. However, global companies with Melbourne offices (Atlassian, Google, Canva) maintain US-style coding interviews.

**Emphasis on culture fit:** "Values-based interviews" are common, where behavioral questions assess alignment with company values. Research the company's values and prepare stories that map to them.

**Relaxed communication style:** Australian professional culture is informal by default. Don't over-formalize your answers. Directness is valued — if you don't know something, say so clearly rather than hedging.

**Technical depth:** Don't mistake informality for low standards. Melbourne engineers are technically strong, particularly in distributed systems, cloud infrastructure, and data engineering (large companies have significant data teams).

## Visa and Work Authorization

**Skilled Worker Visas:**
- **Subclass 482 (TSS):** Employer-sponsored temporary work visa. Most common path for foreign engineers. Requires a sponsoring employer. Can lead to PR.
- **Subclass 189/190:** Skilled Independent and Skilled Nominated visas. Points-based. Software engineers typically qualify (on the Medium Long-term Strategic Skills List). Processing times vary.
- **Working Holiday (Subclass 417/462):** For those under 35 from eligible countries. Good for testing Australia before committing. Limited to one year (extendable to two or three with regional work).

New Zealand citizens have special access under the Trans-Tasman Travel Arrangement.

## Tech Hubs and Neighborhoods

Melbourne's tech scene concentrates in:
- **CBD (Central Business District):** Large enterprise and financial services
- **Cremorne:** Melbourne's unofficial "Silicon Yarra" — startups, tech companies, growing density of engineering offices
- **Fitzroy/Collingwood:** Creative tech, agencies, smaller startups
- **Docklands:** ANZ, NAB, large enterprise campus offices
- **Richmond:** Mix of established tech companies and scale-ups

Remote work is common post-COVID, particularly at established companies. Many engineers commute 2-3 days per week.

## Community and Networking

- **Melbourne Silicon Beach:** Regional tech community group
- **YOW! Conference:** Major Australian developer conference
- **DDD Melbourne:** Developer community conference
- **GovHack:** Annual civic hackathon with Melbourne participation
- **Meetup scene:** Active Python, JavaScript, Go, Kubernetes, and data engineering meetups

Melbourne's engineering community is collaborative and close-knit. Attending meetups is genuinely useful for networking and job searching — many roles are filled through connections before public posting.

## Getting Hired

Apply through Seek (dominant Australian job board), LinkedIn, and company career pages. Referrals are powerful — warm introductions through the community dramatically improve response rates. Smaller Melbourne companies often hire directly from their networks.

For senior/staff roles, reach out to hiring managers directly on LinkedIn. Australian professionals are generally receptive to direct outreach if it's personalized and demonstrates genuine interest.
