# Barcelona Tech Interview Guide: Companies, Culture, and Landing the Job

Barcelona has emerged as one of Europe's most attractive tech hubs, combining a lower cost of living than London or Zurich with a genuinely cosmopolitan engineering culture. The city hosts the headquarters of several significant tech companies and a thriving startup scene, while offering a quality of life that draws engineers from across the EU and beyond.

## Barcelona's Tech Ecosystem

Barcelona's tech scene is distinct from Madrid's in character. Where Madrid leans toward enterprise, banking, and telco, Barcelona skews toward e-commerce, consumer tech, and startups. The Mobile World Congress (held annually in Barcelona) has made the city a natural hub for mobile and connectivity companies.

**Anchor companies:** Glovo (food delivery unicorn), Wallapop (marketplace), Typeform (SaaS forms), Factorial (HR software), TravelPerk (B2B travel), King (Candy Crush, games), and Softonic (software distribution). These are the flagship names on a Barcelona-focused CV.

**Enterprise and tech multinationals:** Amazon, Microsoft, Criteo (AdTech), and Telefónica's innovation arm have significant engineering presence. HP has one of its largest European offices in Sant Cugat (Barcelona metro).

**Consulting and services:** Accenture, Capgemini, and various boutique digital agencies offer less competitive pay but stable employment and often sponsor non-EU visas more readily than product companies.

## Salaries and Compensation

Barcelona salaries are notably lower than comparable roles in Northern Europe, which is the city's main downside as a tech destination.

**Software engineer (3-5 years):** €35,000–50,000 gross annually. Senior engineers with 5+ years: €50,000–70,000. Staff/principal: €70,000–95,000 at the larger product companies.

**Equity:** Startup equity is increasingly common. Glovo and TravelPerk alumni have seen meaningful exits, though European startup equity remains less reliable than US equivalents.

**Benefits:** Spain has strong labor protections. Unlimited PTO is uncommon — 22-23 days plus public holidays is standard. Healthcare contributions are employer-paid. Social security is comprehensive but reduces take-home pay significantly.

**Cost of living offset:** A €45K Barcelona salary buys considerably more than a €55K Paris or €65K Amsterdam salary, given Barcelona's substantially lower rents and living costs.

## The Interview Process

Barcelona company interviews blend Northern European rigor with a more relationship-oriented Mediterranean style. First impressions and cultural fit are weighted more heavily than in, say, German or Dutch companies.

**Typical process at a Barcelona product company:**
1. Recruiter screen (30 min, English or Spanish)
2. Technical phone screen — often a HackerRank or Codility assessment
3. Technical interview (90 min) — LeetCode-style problems, medium difficulty emphasis
4. System design round (senior roles)
5. Culture/values interview with team lead

**Language:** English is the engineering lingua franca at nearly all international companies. Spanish helps for networking and at more locally-rooted companies. Catalan is spoken in the city and valued culturally but rarely required professionally.

**LeetCode level:** Medium difficulty is the standard bar. Startups like Glovo and Typeform emphasize problem-solving process over optimality. FAANG subsidiaries (Amazon) use their standard global process.

## Company-Specific Notes

**Glovo:** Fast-paced, high growth. Engineering interviews emphasize system design (delivery logistics involves hard real-time routing problems) and pragmatic coding. Values ownership and speed over polish.

**Factorial:** Series C HR tech company. Strong engineering culture, emphasizes clean code and domain-driven design. System design rounds focus on multi-tenancy, data isolation, and SaaS patterns.

**TravelPerk:** B2B travel platform, known for strong engineering culture and relatively high Barcelona salaries. Focuses on TypeScript backend, distributed systems for booking aggregation.

**King:** AAA game company (Activision Blizzard subsidiary). Interview process includes game-specific algorithms (pathfinding, state machines) alongside standard SWE questions. Offers Amsterdam-level compensation in Barcelona.

**Typeform:** Design-forward product company, known for good work culture. Full-stack TypeScript. Relatively small engineering team, values craftsperson mentality.

## Visa and Work Authorization

Spain's digital nomad visa (launched 2023) is not a work visa — it covers remote workers employed by foreign companies, not local employment. For local jobs:

**EU/EEA citizens:** No visa required, full work rights immediately.

**Non-EU citizens:** Require an employer-sponsored work permit. Spanish work permits take 3-6 months to process. Most startups are willing to sponsor for senior engineers; junior roles are more difficult.

**Highly Qualified Professional Visa:** For non-EU engineers with job offers above a salary threshold (~€40K+), this is the most common path. The employer handles most of the paperwork.

## Neighborhoods and Practical Considerations

Engineers typically live in Eixample, Gràcia, Poblenou (home to the 22@ tech district), or Sant Martí. The 22@ district has the highest concentration of tech offices and coworking spaces.

Commutes are manageable by European standards. The metro system is efficient; many engineers cycle year-round given Barcelona's climate.

## Networking and Finding Roles

**Jobs boards:** LinkedIn is dominant. InfoJobs is the Spanish equivalent for more locally-rooted companies. Startup Barcelona (startupbarcelona.com) lists startup roles.

**Events:** Barcelona Tech City, Startup Grind Barcelona, various AWS/GCP meetups. Mobile World Congress is the networking event of the year.

**Recruitment agencies:** Michael Page, Hays, and local agencies like Talent Search People are active in Barcelona's market and often receive roles before public posting.

## What Stands Out on a Barcelona CV

- Experience with e-commerce or marketplace platforms (strong demand from Glovo, Wallapop, etc.)
- TypeScript/Node.js or Kotlin/Java for backend
- Experience with distributed systems and real-time data
- English proficiency (often tested directly)
- Any contribution to the Barcelona tech community (meetups, open source, blog)

Barcelona rewards engineers who make the city a real commitment rather than treating it as a temporary post. Companies that have had to rebuild after engineers left for higher salaries elsewhere are attuned to this; cultural fit and intent-to-stay questions are genuine, not pro forma.
