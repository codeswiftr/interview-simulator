# San Francisco Tech Job Market 2026: AI Boom, FAANG, and the New Landscape

San Francisco's tech market in 2026 is defined by a striking bifurcation: an AI hiring boom that rivals the 2021 peak, running alongside a stabilized (but not fully recovered) market at established tech companies. Understanding which world you're entering matters enormously for your job search strategy.

## The Two SF Tech Markets

**The AI boom:** Anthropic, OpenAI, Cohere, Mistral, and dozens of AI infrastructure companies are hiring aggressively. These companies are at the frontier of a genuine technology transition and compensation reflects the competition for talent. AI researchers and ML engineers at top labs command packages that exceed traditional FAANG levels — $400K-$800K+ total compensation is real at the most competitive end. AI product engineers, inference infrastructure engineers, and applied AI scientists are in extremely high demand.

**The established tech market:** Post-2022/2023 layoffs, the major tech companies have stabilized at leaner headcounts. Google, Meta, Amazon, and Apple are still hiring, but with more selective bars and less redundant headcount. A senior engineer who would have gotten offers from 4 of 5 companies in 2021 might get 2 of 5 today. The market is competitive but functional — not the bonanza of 2021 or the freeze of early 2023.

## Salary Ranges (2026)

SF commands the highest absolute salaries of any tech market globally:
- Junior engineer (0-3 yr): $130K–$180K base, + equity
- Mid-level (3-6 yr): $180K–$250K base, + equity
- Senior engineer (6-10 yr): $250K–$350K base, + equity
- Staff/principal: $350K–$600K+ base, + substantial equity

At AI labs, total comp for ML engineers can reach $400K–$800K+ when equity is included, especially at pre-IPO companies with near-term liquidity events.

**The equity component:** SF compensation packages are heavily weighted toward equity — often 30-50% of total compensation at growth-stage companies. The upside is real; so is the risk that private company equity becomes worth less than expected.

## Cost of Living Reality

SF remains one of the world's most expensive cities. Benchmarks for 2026:

- 1-bedroom apartment: $2,800–$4,500/month (city), $2,200–$3,500 (East Bay/South Bay commute)
- Combined state + federal income tax for senior engineers: effective rate 40-45%
- Monthly burn rate for a comfortable lifestyle: $7,000–$12,000 for a single person

The math: a senior engineer at $300K base keeps ~$165K–$180K after federal + CA state taxes. Monthly take-home of ~$14K–$15K, with $7K–$10K in living expenses, leaves meaningful savings — but less than the headline number suggests.

Many engineers optimize by living in Oakland, Berkeley, or the South Bay, where rent is 20-40% lower and commute is manageable.

## H-1B Visa Reality

The H-1B visa cap makes SF a difficult market for engineers requiring sponsorship in the near term. The annual cap (65K regular + 20K master's cap) is massively oversubscribed — lottery odds are roughly 30-40% per year. This means:

- Engineers currently on H-1B from large companies (already sponsored) are relatively protected
- New-to-US engineers face a 2-4 year delay to get H-1B approval through the lottery
- Some companies (Anthropic, OpenAI, Stripe) aggressively sponsor H-1B and help with premium processing
- O-1 visa (extraordinary ability) is available for candidates with strong research/publication records

Engineers with green cards or US citizenship have a significant advantage in SF compensation negotiations.

## When SF Makes Sense

SF is the right market if:
- You're targeting an AI lab or AI-first company where physical presence near the team matters
- You want to maximize equity upside on pre-IPO shares
- Your network is in the Bay Area and you want density of top engineers around you
- You're at the very top of the technical market and want maximum compensation leverage

Consider remote or other cities if:
- Your role is fully remote-capable (many SF companies now hire remotely for non-AI roles)
- The cost-of-living math doesn't work for your situation
- You're at a stage where quality of life matters more than absolute compensation
