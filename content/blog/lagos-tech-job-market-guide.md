# Lagos Tech Job Market Guide 2025: Nigeria's Silicon Valley

Lagos is Africa's largest city and its most important tech hub. The ecosystem that grew up here—anchored by fintech giants, accelerated by diaspora talent returning from the US and UK, and increasingly funded by global VCs—has made Lagos a genuinely competitive destination for tech talent. Whether you're a Nigerian engineer considering your options, a diaspora professional thinking about returning, or an international tech worker curious about the market, this guide breaks down what you actually need to know about working in Lagos tech in 2025.

## The Lagos Tech Ecosystem: Key Players and Sectors

Lagos's tech scene is defined by fintech above all else. Nigeria's combination of a massive unbanked population, a diaspora with significant remittance flows, and a young, mobile-first demographic created the conditions for fintech to become the dominant tech vertical. The companies that emerged from this environment are now regional and global players.

**Flutterwave** is Nigeria's most valuable startup, providing payment infrastructure across Africa. Its engineers work on systems that process millions of transactions daily across dozens of currencies and markets. The technical challenges—multi-currency reconciliation, fraud detection at scale, regulatory compliance across different African markets—are genuinely hard problems.

**Paystack**, acquired by Stripe in 2020 for approximately $200 million, remains one of the most desirable employers in the Lagos market. The Stripe acquisition brought global engineering culture and compensation practices to the Lagos team, which has made Paystack a benchmark for what competitive tech employment looks like in Nigeria. The engineering bar is high; the compensation reflects it.

**Andela** operates differently from the others—it's a talent marketplace that connects African engineers with global companies rather than a product company. For engineers who want to work on global products while remaining in Lagos (or anywhere in Africa), Andela provides a path. The company has placed tens of thousands of engineers with companies ranging from small startups to Fortune 500s.

Beyond these marquee names, the ecosystem includes **Opay** (super app and financial services), **Kuda** (digital banking), **PiggyVest** (savings and investment), **Cowrywise** (investment platform), and a growing number of B2B SaaS companies targeting African enterprises. Healthtech, edtech, and logistics are emerging verticals—**Helium Health** in healthtech and **Kobo360** in logistics are examples of companies building significant engineering teams.

## Salaries: What to Expect in NGN and USD

Salary conversations in Lagos tech require understanding two parallel realities: naira-denominated salaries at local companies and dollar-denominated salaries at companies with global backing or remote-first models.

For local companies paying in naira, mid-level software engineers at established startups typically earn in the range of ₦4 million to ₦10 million annually (approximately $2,500–$6,500 USD at current rates). Senior engineers at top-tier companies like Paystack or Flutterwave can earn ₦15 million to ₦30 million or more, which at current exchange rates is $10,000–$20,000 USD—modest by US standards but substantial in Lagos's cost environment.

The more interesting salary tier is dollar-denominated remote work. Engineers working for US, UK, or European companies while based in Lagos—either through employers of record, contractor arrangements, or companies like Andela—typically earn $30,000–$80,000 USD for senior roles. This is transformative in the Lagos context, where a $50,000 USD annual salary provides a standard of living that would require $150,000+ in San Francisco or London.

The naira's volatility is a real consideration. Engineers at naira-paying companies have seen significant purchasing power erosion during periods of currency depreciation. This has accelerated the demand for dollar-denominated employment among Nigerian engineers and driven significant growth in the remote-work segment.

## How to Get Hired in Lagos Tech

The Lagos hiring market operates through a mix of channels that differ meaningfully from Western tech markets.

**LinkedIn** is the primary professional networking platform and recruiter channel. A well-optimized LinkedIn profile is essential. Nigerian tech recruiters are active on the platform, and many senior roles are filled through recruiter outreach before they're ever posted publicly.

**Community and referrals** matter enormously. The Lagos tech community is tight-knit—communities like **She Code Africa**, **Ingressive for Good**, and **Techpoint Africa's** network connect engineers with opportunities and mentors. Developer communities organized around specific technologies (Nigeria's JavaScript, Python, and React communities are active) also serve as job market networks.

**Direct applications** to company career pages work for well-known companies, but the volume of applications at places like Flutterwave means standing out requires either a strong referral or an impressive portfolio. Open source contributions, technical blog posts, and visible work in the community all help.

For engineers targeting remote work, platforms like **Turing**, **Toptal**, and **Remote.com** have established presences in the Nigerian market. Andela's marketplace is specifically built for this use case.

## Remote Work Opportunities and the Global Market

One of the most significant developments in the Lagos tech market over the past five years is the normalization of remote work for Nigerian engineers at global companies. The pandemic accelerated this trend, and it hasn't reversed.

Nigerian engineers are increasingly competitive for roles at US and European companies, particularly in engineering disciplines like backend development, data engineering, and mobile development. The time zone (WAT, UTC+1) is workable for collaboration with European teams and manageable for US East Coast overlap with some schedule flexibility.

Interview preparation for global roles requires attention to the same topics that matter anywhere—system design, data structures and algorithms, behavioral interviews—but also awareness of how to position a Lagos-based career narrative for international audiences. Emphasizing scale (Nigeria's 220 million population means fintech and consumer tech systems serve large user bases), global standards exposure (many Nigerian companies use the same tools and practices as global tech companies), and communication skills are all important.

AI interview simulators can be particularly valuable here because they allow engineers to practice in conditions that mirror global company interview formats, with feedback calibrated to international hiring standards. The ability to rehearse system design explanations or behavioral responses without geographic constraints is a genuine advantage for Lagos-based engineers targeting global opportunities.

Lagos tech is no longer a local story. The engineers, companies, and capital flowing through this ecosystem are increasingly shaping technology across the African continent and beyond.
