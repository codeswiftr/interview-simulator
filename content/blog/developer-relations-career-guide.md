# Developer Relations Career Guide: Becoming a Developer Advocate

Developer relations (DevRel) sits at the intersection of engineering, community, and product. It's one of the most unique career paths in tech — part engineer, part educator, part community builder — and it's growing as API-first and developer-tool companies proliferate.

## What Developer Relations Actually Is

DevRel is a broad term that encompasses several related roles:

**Developer Advocate**: The most common DevRel title. Advocates create content (blog posts, videos, tutorials, talks), build sample applications, speak at conferences, engage with developer communities, and represent the developer perspective internally. Heavy public-facing component.

**Developer Experience (DX) Engineer**: More internal-facing. Focused on improving the tooling, documentation, SDKs, and onboarding experience for the platform. Works closely with product and engineering teams. More technical, less content creation.

**Community Manager**: Manages developer communities — Discord servers, forums, GitHub discussions. Focuses on engagement, support, and building a sense of belonging around a platform.

**Technical Evangelist**: A more external-facing role that emphasizes sales/partnership contexts — presenting at enterprise events, working with strategic partners, showcasing the platform at industry conferences.

In practice, these roles blend together, especially at smaller companies where one person handles all of it.

## Is DevRel Right for You?

DevRel attracts engineers who love building AND communicating. The profile of a successful DevRel engineer:

- Genuinely enjoys teaching and helping others
- Has the ability to explain complex technical concepts to different audiences (beginners to experts)
- Enjoys the variety of travel, content creation, community engagement, and technical work
- Can switch between deep technical work and public-facing communication comfortably
- Is self-directed — DevRel roles have high autonomy and ambiguous success metrics

**Honest tradeoffs**: DevRel involves significant travel (pre-COVID, 30–50% of time; post-COVID, less but still real). It can feel scattered — you're not building one cohesive product but rather touching many things at once. Career ladders are less well-defined than engineering tracks.

## Technical Requirements

DevRel requires real technical credibility. The specific skills depend on the platform:

**For backend/API platforms (Stripe, Twilio, SendGrid)**:
- Strong programming in 2–3 languages (typically Python, JavaScript/Node, and one more)
- Understanding of REST and GraphQL API design
- Familiarity with authentication (OAuth, API keys, JWTs)
- Ability to build end-to-end integrations from scratch

**For cloud platforms (AWS, GCP, Azure)**:
- Cloud architecture fundamentals
- Infrastructure-as-code tools (Terraform, CDK)
- Containerization (Docker, Kubernetes basics)

**For ML/AI platforms (OpenAI, Anthropic, Hugging Face)**:
- Python proficiency
- Understanding of ML model concepts (even if not training models)
- API integration and prompt engineering
- Familiarity with Jupyter notebooks and data science workflows

**The bar**: You need to be credible building real applications with the technology you're advocating. If you can't build a non-trivial demo in a few hours, the technical credibility gap will be visible.

## Content Creation Skills

A substantial part of DevRel is content: technical blog posts, video tutorials, documentation, conference talks, and sample applications. You'll need:

**Writing**: Clear technical writing — explaining concepts step by step, writing getting-started tutorials, documenting APIs. Grammarly and clear prose structure matter.

**Public speaking**: Conference talks are a major DevRel channel. This is a learnable skill — start with local meetups, then regional conferences, then major ones.

**Video**: Video tutorials for YouTube/platform docs are increasingly important. You don't need professional production quality, but clear screen recordings with good audio matter.

**Sample apps**: Building complete, polished example applications that showcase the platform's capabilities. These need to be actually good code — developers will read it.

## Breaking Into DevRel

### Path from Software Engineering

This is the most common path. To transition:

1. **Start creating content** before you're hired into DevRel. Write blog posts, give meetup talks, build demo apps, contribute to documentation.
2. **Build a public presence** — a technical blog, conference talk recordings on YouTube, or a GitHub portfolio of integrations/demos.
3. **Engage with developer communities** around the platform you want to advocate for — be known in the community before applying.

### Path from Technical Writing

Technical writers who develop strong coding skills often move into DevRel naturally. The writing ability is a significant advantage.

### Developer Advocates as Your First Job

Some companies hire junior DevRel engineers directly from school or bootcamps, but it's rare. Most DevRel roles require 2–5 years of software engineering experience to have the technical credibility to be effective.

## Compensation and Career Growth

**Compensation**: DevRel pays less than equivalent engineering roles at most companies, though this varies:

| Level | Typical Range |
|-------|--------------|
| Junior Developer Advocate | $90K–$130K |
| Developer Advocate | $130K–$180K |
| Senior Developer Advocate | $170K–$230K |
| Director of DevRel | $200K–$280K |

**Career paths from DevRel**:
- Return to software engineering (strengthened by the writing and communication skills)
- Move into product management (DevRel gives excellent product intuition)
- Build a personal brand as a content creator/educator
- Leadership roles: Head of DevRel, VP of Community

For engineers who want to be close to users, communicate technical concepts publicly, and build community around technology they love, DevRel is one of the most fulfilling career paths available.
