# Best Questions to Ask Interviewers: 35 Questions That Impress

"Do you have any questions for us?" is not a formality — it's an evaluation criterion. The questions you ask reveal your priorities, how you think about engineering work, and how seriously you've considered this particular role. Strong candidates use this time to gather decision-relevant information and to demonstrate the sophistication of their thinking. This guide gives you 35 excellent questions organized by category.

## Questions About Engineering Culture and Process

These reveal how the team actually works, not how they describe their process:

1. "What does your on-call rotation look like, and how much sleep interruption do engineers typically experience in a month?"

2. "How does the team handle technical disagreements — can you give me an example of a recent one and how it was resolved?"

3. "What's your current test coverage, and how does the team enforce quality standards in PRs?"

4. "What does a typical sprint or planning cycle look like? How much time do engineers have for technical improvement vs. feature work?"

5. "How does the team decide when to take on technical debt vs. when to address it?"

6. "What's the relationship between engineering and product here — who has final say on technical feasibility tradeoffs?"

7. "How do you onboard new engineers? What does the first 90 days look like?"

8. "How often do engineers ship to production, and what does the deployment process look like?"

## Questions About Team Dynamics and People

These help you evaluate the team you'll actually work with:

9. "How would you describe the best engineers on this team? What makes them effective?"

10. "What's the balance of senior to junior engineers on the team, and how does that affect how work is structured?"

11. "How long have you been on this team, and what's kept you here?"

12. "What types of engineers tend to thrive here, and what types tend to struggle?"

13. "How does the team handle failure — if someone ships a bug that causes a significant incident, what happens?"

14. "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

## Questions About Technical Stack and Architecture

These demonstrate your technical curiosity and reveal the actual state of the codebase:

15. "What's the biggest area of technical debt in the system you work on, and is there a plan to address it?"

16. "What architectural decisions are you currently debating or reconsidering?"

17. "How does the system perform at peak load, and what are the current scaling bottlenecks?"

18. "What's the age of the oldest major component in the system, and what's its current state?"

19. "How do you handle observability — what does an engineer look at when investigating a production issue at 2am?"

20. "What's the testing strategy — unit tests, integration tests, E2E? Where are the gaps?"

## Questions About Growth and Career Development

These reveal whether the company invests in engineers:

21. "Can you tell me about an engineer who joined at this level and what their career progression looked like over two years?"

22. "How does the promotion process work? What's the typical timeline from [target level] to the next level?"

23. "Is there a dedicated budget for conference attendance, courses, or certifications? How is that typically used?"

24. "Does the company support open-source contributions or speaking at conferences? Are there examples?"

25. "How does the team transfer knowledge — are there regular tech talks, architecture reviews, or learning sessions?"

## Questions About the Role and Expectations

These clarify what success actually looks like:

26. "What would make someone hired for this role extremely successful in their first 90 days?"

27. "What's the most important problem the person in this role needs to solve in the first six months?"

28. "How does this team's work connect to company-level priorities and revenue?"

29. "Are there any concerns about my background or experience that you'd like to discuss?"

30. "What does the next year look like for this team — is the scope expanding, contracting, or stabilizing?"

## Questions for Engineering Managers (Not Engineers)

These probe leadership philosophy and team health:

31. "How do you think about career development for engineers on your team?"

32. "What's your philosophy on how much autonomy engineers should have vs. how prescriptive you are about approach?"

33. "How do you handle situations where an engineer is struggling — what's your process?"

34. "What's the single biggest thing you're trying to improve about how this team works?"

35. "What's your management style, and how does it fit with the team's current needs?"

## How to Use These Questions Effectively

**Don't ask all of them**: Select 3-5 questions per interviewer. Asking 15 questions signals poor preparation and no filter.

**Prioritize based on your concerns**: What actually matters to you? Work-life balance? Technical depth? Career growth? Prioritize questions that address your real concerns.

**Use "tell me about a time" framing**: "How does the team handle X?" gets better answers than "Does the team do X?" Open questions reveal the actual culture.

**Listen for what's not said**: If a question about technical debt is deflected with "we're always improving," that tells you something. If someone gives a 5-minute enthusiastic answer about a hard problem they solved, that tells you something different.

**Build on previous answers**: The best conversations feel like interviews, not questionnaires. "You mentioned X earlier — that connects to what I was going to ask about Y..."

The questions you ask are as much a part of your interview performance as the coding round. Engineers who ask thoughtful, specific questions demonstrate that they've thought carefully about what makes an engineering role genuinely good — and companies want to hire people who think that way.
