# The 30-Minute Daily Routine That Landed Me 3 FAANG Offers

*Why consistency beats intensity—and the exact framework that works.*

---

Let me tell you about two engineers preparing for FAANG interviews.

**Engineer A** takes two weeks off work. They grind 8 hours a day. LeetCode from dawn to midnight. By day 10, they're burned out, anxious, and starting to hate coding.

**Engineer B** spends 30 minutes every morning before work. Same time, same place, same routine. For three months. They land offers from Google, Meta, and Amazon.

The difference isn't talent. It's **consistency over intensity**.

I was Engineer B. Here's exactly what I did.

---

## Why 30 Minutes Works Better Than 4 Hours

Before I share the routine, let's talk about why this approach works:

### 1. Spaced Repetition > Massed Practice

Your brain doesn't form strong memories in marathon sessions. It forms them through **repeated exposure over time**.

Solving one medium problem every day for 90 days beats solving 90 problems in one week. The forgetting curve is real.

### 2. Sustainable Beats Heroic

Most people can't maintain 4-hour daily study sessions while working full-time. They do it for a week, burn out, take a month off, then panic-cram before interviews.

30 minutes is small enough that you'll actually do it. Every day. Even when you're tired.

### 3. Decision Fatigue Is Real

When your study plan is "do LeetCode," you spend mental energy deciding *what* to practice. By the time you've picked a problem, you're already drained.

A fixed routine eliminates decisions. You just execute.

## The Exact 30-Minute Framework

Here's the routine, broken into three 10-minute blocks:

### Block 1: Pattern Recognition (10 minutes)

**Goal**: Identify the problem type and recall the standard approach.

1. Read a new problem (choose randomly from a curated list—more on this below)
2. **Don't solve it yet.** Just identify:
   - What category is this? (Two pointers? BFS? Dynamic programming?)
   - What's the standard template for this category?
   - What are the likely edge cases?
3. Write one sentence describing your approach

**Why this works**: In real interviews, pattern recognition is 70% of the battle. If you can identify "this is a sliding window problem" in 30 seconds, you've already won.

### Block 2: Active Coding (10 minutes)

**Goal**: Implement the solution with a hard time limit.

1. Set a timer for exactly 10 minutes
2. Code the solution
3. If you don't finish, that's fine. Note where you got stuck.

**Why this works**: Time pressure in practice creates time comfort in interviews. Most medium problems should be solvable in 15-20 minutes. You're training speed.

### Block 3: Review and Verbalize (10 minutes)

**Goal**: Solidify learning and practice communication.

1. If you solved it: Explain your solution out loud. Walk through time/space complexity.
2. If you didn't: Read the optimal solution. Understand *why* it works, not just *how*.
3. Write one takeaway in a personal notes file

**Why this works**: You're encoding the memory twice—once by doing, once by explaining. And you're practicing the verbal communication that most people neglect.

## The Problem Selection Strategy

Don't just grab random LeetCode problems. You need a systematic approach.

### The "75 List" Foundation

Start with a curated list like [Blind 75](https://www.teamblind.com/post/New-Year-Gift---Curated-List-of-Top-75-LeetCode-Questions-to-Save-Your-Time-OaM1orEU) or [NeetCode 150](https://neetcode.io/). These lists are optimized for pattern coverage, not volume.

### Category Rotation

Don't grind one topic for a week. Rotate daily:

- Monday: Arrays/Strings
- Tuesday: Trees
- Wednesday: Graphs
- Thursday: Dynamic Programming
- Friday: Sliding Window/Two Pointers
- Saturday: Backtracking/Recursion
- Sunday: Review weakest area from the week

This interleaving is harder in the moment but produces better long-term retention.

### Difficulty Progression

- Week 1-4: 80% Easy, 20% Medium
- Week 5-8: 50% Easy, 50% Medium
- Week 9-12: 20% Easy, 70% Medium, 10% Hard

Don't touch Hard problems until you can solve most Mediums in 15 minutes.

## The Weekly Add-Ons

The daily 30-minute routine is the core. But once a week, add these:

### Saturday: One Full Mock Interview (45 minutes)

Do a complete mock interview with realistic conditions:
- Timer running
- Camera on (even if no one's watching)
- Verbalize everything

### Sunday: Review and Planning (20 minutes)

- Review the week's notes
- Identify your weakest pattern
- Adjust next week's rotation if needed

## Sample Day in Practice

**7:00 AM**: Coffee. Open laptop to pre-selected problem.

**7:00-7:10**: Read problem. Think. "This is a BFS problem—I need to track visited nodes and process level by level. Edge case: disconnected graph."

**7:10-7:20**: Code. Timer running. Get the solution working with one minute to spare.

**7:20-7:30**: "My solution uses BFS with a queue. Time complexity is O(V + E) where V is vertices and E is edges. Space is O(V) for the visited set. If I had more time, I'd consider whether DFS is more space-efficient here."

**7:30**: Close laptop. Go to work.

That's it. 30 minutes. Every day.

## Common Objections (And Answers)

### "30 minutes isn't enough time to deeply understand anything."

Wrong. Deep understanding comes from **repeated exposure**, not marathon sessions. You'll revisit the same patterns dozens of times over three months.

### "I don't have 30 minutes in the morning."

Wake up 30 minutes earlier. Or do it during lunch. Or right after work. The specific time doesn't matter—the consistency does.

### "What if I'm already interviewing in 2 weeks?"

Then do 30 minutes in the morning AND 30 minutes in the evening. But don't grind for 6 hours. You'll burn out and interview worse, not better.

### "I've been doing this for a year and I'm not improving."

Then you're practicing wrong, not practicing too little. Are you identifying patterns? Verbalizing? Reviewing mistakes? Or just solving problems on autopilot?
