# The Psychology of "Performance Anxiety" in Coding

*Why your brain shuts down during interviews and how to hack it back online.*

---

You code perfectly fine at home. You built a complex side project last weekend. But put a stranger in front of you and a timer on the screen, and suddenly you can't write a `for` loop.

This isn't a lack of skill. It's **Performance Anxiety**. And it's pure biology.

## The Amygdala Hijack

When you perceive a threat (like a judgmental interviewer), your amygdala activates the "Fight or Flight" response.
*   **Cortisol** floods your system.
*   **Heart rate** increases.
*   **Blood flow** moves from your brain to your muscles (so you can run away from the lion).

The problem? You need that blood in your **Prefrontal Cortex** (PFC). The PFC handles logic, working memory, and complex problem-solving.

When the amygdala screams, the PFC shuts up. You literally become stupider.

## How to Hack Your Biology

You can't stop the adrenaline, but you can manage it.

### 1. Reframe the Threat
Your body doesn't know the difference between "a bear is chasing me" and "I might fail this interview."
**The Hack**: Cognitive Reappraisal. Tell yourself: "I am excited," not "I am nervous." The physiological symptoms (racing heart) are identical. Reframe the energy as focus.

### 2. Exposure Therapy
The more you do something scary, the less scary it gets.
**The Hack**: Do mock interviews until they become boring. If you only interview once a year, the stakes feel huge. If you interview 10 times a week, it becomes routine.

### 3. Box Breathing
Navy SEALs use this to stay calm in combat.
**The Hack**:
*   Inhale for 4 seconds.
*   Hold for 4 seconds.
*   Exhale for 4 seconds.
*   Hold for 4 seconds.
This forces your parasympathetic nervous system to kick in and lower your heart rate.

### 4. Reduce Cognitive Load
Your working memory is limited. Trying to hold the whole problem in your head *plus* your anxiety is too much.
**The Hack**: Write everything down. Offload your memory to the whiteboard/editor.
