# Dublin Tech Job Market: Engineering Careers in Europe's Silicon Valley

Dublin has quietly become one of the world's most important tech cities — not through building world-famous products, but by becoming the European headquarters for virtually every major American tech company. Google, Meta, Apple, LinkedIn, Stripe, Salesforce, HubSpot, and dozens more have chosen Ireland as their EMEA base. For engineers, this creates an extraordinary concentration of high-paying, high-caliber roles in a compact, English-speaking city.

## Why Dublin? The EMEA Hub Story

Ireland's appeal to US tech companies is well-documented: favorable corporate tax rates (12.5% vs 20-25% elsewhere in Europe), English language, EU membership, access to European talent, and an established tech ecosystem that makes hiring easier. The result is that Dublin's tech jobs often come with global scope — you might be on a team of 50 people responsible for a product used by 500 million Europeans.

**The major employers:**

*The "FAANG-and-friends" EMEA headquarters:*
- **Google** (Dublin is EMEA HQ, 8,000+ employees) — Engineering, infrastructure, trust and safety, advertising tech
- **Meta** (major EMEA base, several thousand engineers) — Infrastructure, content moderation systems, ads
- **LinkedIn** (major engineering hub) — Platform engineering, data infrastructure
- **Apple** (European HQ in Cork, significant Dublin presence) — Siri, Apple Pay European operations, software QA
- **Microsoft** (EU data center operations, engineering teams)

*Fintech powerhouses:*
- **Stripe** — Dublin is co-headquarters (alongside San Francisco). Significant engineering teams in payments infrastructure, reliability, developer platform
- **Workday** — Major EMEA engineering hub; enterprise software, financial systems
- **Mastercard/Visa** — European tech operations
- **AIB/Bank of Ireland/Revolut** — Irish banks plus European fintechs with significant engineering

*Irish tech companies:*
- **Intercom** — Product-led growth company, founded in Dublin, still engineering-heavy there
- **Personio** — German HR tech with Dublin engineering hub
- **Wayflyer/Flipdish/Pointy** — Irish startups that have scaled significantly

## Salary Ranges and Compensation

Dublin salaries have risen sharply over the past decade and now rival London for top-tier roles, without London's cost of living premium.

**Typical ranges (2026):**
- Junior engineer (0-3 years): €50K–€80K
- Mid-level engineer (3-6 years): €80K–€130K
- Senior engineer (6-10 years): €130K–€180K
- Staff/principal engineer: €180K–€280K+

**FAANG premium:** Google, Meta, and LinkedIn pay at the top of the market. A mid-level Google engineer in Dublin might earn €120K base + €60-100K RSU annual vesting + €20K bonus — total comp €200K+. These packages rival San Francisco in absolute terms while Dublin's cost of living is lower (though still high by European standards).

**Stock compensation:** US-listed companies grant RSUs in USD, which means Dublin engineers participate in the same equity upside as their San Francisco counterparts. This is a significant advantage over working for European companies with private equity or no equity component.

**Tax reality:** Ireland's income tax is substantial at higher incomes — effective rates of 40-50% on income above €40K. Engineers gross-to-net at senior levels often keep 55-60% of base salary. However, US company RSUs are taxed on vesting, and Ireland has a SARP (Special Assignee Relief Program) for international hires that can reduce tax burden for the first 5 years.

## The Interview Process

For US companies' Dublin offices, the interview process is typically identical to their global standard:

**Google Dublin:** Same Googleyness/googliness process — coding (2-3 rounds of algorithm problems), system design (1-2 rounds), behavioral/leadership. Hiring committees, compensation leveling, and offer processes are globally consistent.

**Meta Dublin:** Similar to US Meta process — coding, system design, behavioral. Dublin often interviews for roles that are globally posted; you might interview alongside candidates for Singapore, London, and Austin simultaneously.

**Stripe Dublin:** Stripe's engineering bar is high regardless of location. Expect a home assignment (take-home project), followed by technical deep-dives and system design. Stripe values writing ability; expect to discuss a technical document you've written.

**Irish companies (Intercom, etc.):** More European-style process — typically 3-4 rounds, combination of technical and cultural. Less algorithmic-puzzle-focused than US FAANG.

## Visa and Work Authorization

**EU/EEA/Swiss citizens:** No visa required. Full right to work. Ireland is extremely accessible for European engineers.

**Non-EU citizens:** Critical Path — Ireland's **Critical Skills Employment Permit** covers software engineers earning €38,000+ annually. Applications take 4-8 weeks. Many US companies with Dublin offices have established immigration support teams that streamline this process.

**Stamp 4** (long-term residency): After 2 years on Critical Skills permit, eligible for Stamp 4 — broad work authorization with no employer dependency.

**Post-study option:** If you graduate from an Irish university, you can get a 24-month stay for job hunting (Graduate Scheme).

## Living in Dublin: The Real Picture

Dublin is genuinely expensive — housing costs are the most significant concern. Renting a 1-bedroom apartment in the city center runs €2,000–€2,800/month in 2026. Many engineers commute from neighboring towns (Malahide, Swords, Bray) for lower rents.

The upside: Dublin is compact (walkable or cyclable for many), culturally rich, English-speaking, and well-connected to the rest of Europe. Budget airlines make weekend trips to London, Amsterdam, or Barcelona trivial. The tech community is tight-knit — you'll meet the same engineers across companies at meetups.

For engineers seeking global scope, US-company comp packages, and a high-quality English-speaking city in the EU, Dublin remains one of Europe's most compelling destinations.
