# 12-Week Technical Interview Preparation Plan: From Zero to Offer-Ready

Most engineers underestimate how long technical interview preparation takes. The common mistake is cramming — two weeks of intense LeetCode before applying — and then wondering why the interviews don't go well. A 12-week structured plan builds the right habits, covers all domains, and gives you time to review and strengthen weak areas.

## Week 1-2: Foundation

**Algorithms:** Review Big O notation. Practice array manipulation, string operations, basic sorting. Do 2-3 easy problems per day. Goal: be comfortable analyzing time and space complexity before you start hard problems.

**Data structures review:** Hash maps, arrays, strings, linked lists. You should be able to implement a linked list reversal and a hash map from scratch. These appear as warm-ups in phone screens.

**Behavioral prep start:** Write down your 5-10 most significant professional experiences — projects, challenges, leadership moments, failures. You'll use these as raw material for STAR-format answers throughout preparation.

## Week 3-4: Core Algorithms

**Focus:** Two pointers, sliding window, binary search, recursion basics. These appear in medium problems and are foundational for harder patterns.

**LeetCode targets:** 2 mediums per day. Choose problems by tag — all "two pointer" problems in one session, all "binary search" in the next. Pattern recognition is the goal.

**System design intro:** Read "System Design Interview" by Alex Xu (Part 1). Understand URL shortener, rate limiter, consistent hashing. Don't try to memorize — understand the reasoning behind design choices.

**Mock interviews:** Do your first mock interview with a peer or on Pramp. Talking through solutions out loud is a separate skill from solving them silently — start building it now.

## Week 5-6: Intermediate Data Structures

**Focus:** Trees (BFS, DFS, binary search trees), heaps, stacks and queues, basic graphs (adjacency list, BFS, DFS). These are the most frequently tested non-trivial data structures.

**Trees:** Practice preorder, inorder, postorder traversal (both recursive and iterative). Level-order BFS. Common tree problems: lowest common ancestor, validate BST, serialize/deserialize.

**Graphs:** Connected components, cycle detection, basic shortest path with BFS. 

**LeetCode:** 2-3 mediums per day, mixing tree and graph problems. Track problems in a spreadsheet — note whether you got it on first try, needed hints, or had to look up the solution.

**Behavioral:** Practice STAR answers out loud for 5 scenarios: biggest technical challenge, project failure, disagreement with a teammate, leadership example, most impactful project. Record yourself and review.

## Week 7-8: Advanced Algorithms

**Focus:** Dynamic programming, backtracking, advanced graphs (Dijkstra, topological sort, union-find).

**Dynamic programming:** Start with the classic problems — coin change, longest common subsequence, 0/1 knapsack. Learn to identify the subproblem and state. DP is the hardest topic for most candidates; spend more time here if needed.

**Backtracking:** Permutations, combinations, N-Queens, word search. The template is always the same: choose, explore, unchoose (backtrack).

**System design:** Rate limiter design in depth. Design a key-value store. Understand CAP theorem and consistency models. Practice explaining designs out loud — system design is 40% communication.

## Week 9-10: Company-Specific Prep

**Focus:** Research each target company's interview format, common questions, and engineering blog.

**LeetCode company tags:** Most problems are tagged by company (requires premium, but lists are available online). Do the top 30 problems for each company you're targeting.

**System design:** Complete designs for: social media feed, notification system, ride-sharing backend, distributed cache. For each, know the bottlenecks, scaling strategies, and tradeoffs.

**Behavioral:** Research each company's leadership principles or values. Prepare specific answers that map to their framework (Amazon's Leadership Principles require different preparation than Google's Googleyness questions).

**Mock interviews:** 3+ mocks this week, including at least one full-loop simulation (coding + system design + behavioral back-to-back).

## Week 11: Refinement

**Focus:** Identify and fix weak spots from mock interviews. Review all problems you couldn't solve on first attempt. 

**Practice under pressure:** Time yourself strictly — 35 minutes for a LeetCode medium. Practice starting to code before the full solution is clear (sometimes you need to code to think).

**Communication drills:** Practice narrating your thought process while coding. "I'm going to start with a brute force approach, which is O(n²). Then I'll think about how to optimize..." This think-out-loud habit is what interviewers evaluate.

## Week 12: Final Prep

**Light LeetCode:** 1-2 easy/medium per day for maintenance. No new patterns.

**Review notes:** Go through your spreadsheet of missed problems. Re-solve the ones that tripped you up.

**Logistics:** Confirm interview schedules, test your technical setup (camera, IDE, whiteboard tool), prepare questions to ask interviewers.

**Rest:** Take at least 1-2 full rest days the week before interviews. You can't interview well when exhausted.

## Daily Schedule Template

30 minutes: warm-up (1 easy problem or review previous day's problem)
60 minutes: new problems (2 mediums or 1 hard)
20 minutes: system design reading or notes
10 minutes: behavioral answer refinement

Total: ~2 hours/day. This is sustainable for 12 weeks without burnout.

## Tracking Progress

Keep a preparation spreadsheet with columns: Date, Problem, Difficulty, Status (solved/needed hint/looked up), Pattern, Notes. Review weekly — you should see your "needed hint" rate decreasing and your "first try" rate increasing. If not, shift more time to review rather than new problems.

Preparation is a marathon. Consistent effort over 12 weeks beats cramming every time.
