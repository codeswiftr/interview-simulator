# 90-Day Interview Preparation Plan: From Cold Start to Job Offer

Most interview prep fails because it's random. Engineers grind LeetCode problems without a structure, spend too much time on topics that rarely appear, and don't practice the way interviews actually run. A structured 90-day plan changes this. Here's how to use 90 days to prepare for senior software engineering interviews efficiently.

## The 90-Day Framework

Three phases, each one month:

- **Month 1:** Foundation — DSA fundamentals, resume, target list
- **Month 2:** Building — System design, behavioral stories, mock interviews begin
- **Month 3:** Peaking — Full mock interviews, specific company prep, offer evaluation

The goal isn't to spend every waking hour on prep. Two to three focused hours per day is sustainable and more effective than all-day grinding that leads to fatigue and poor retention.

## Month 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-4)

**Week 1 — Audit and baseline.** Solve 10-15 problems on LeetCode to assess where you stand. Categorize your weak areas. Write or update your resume. Create your target company list (10-15 companies, mix of reach/target/safety).

**Week 2-3 — Core DSA patterns.** Cover the patterns that appear in 80% of interviews: arrays/strings (two pointers, sliding window), hash maps, linked lists, trees (DFS, BFS), and binary search. Solve 3-5 problems per pattern. Focus on pattern recognition, not memorization.

**Week 4 — Harder patterns + review.** Add dynamic programming fundamentals (top-down vs bottom-up, identify subproblem structure), graphs (DFS/BFS on adjacency lists), and heaps/priority queues. Review week 2-3 patterns with new problems.

Metrics for Month 1 completion: comfortable with easy problems (< 15 min), can attempt mediums independently, have a clean resume.

## Month 2: Building (Weeks 5-8)

**Week 5 — System design fundamentals.** Study the core concepts: load balancing, caching, database scaling, CDN, message queues. Read the system design primer. Understand CAP theorem, consistency models, and common tradeoffs.

**Week 6 — System design practice.** Practice designing 4-6 complete systems: URL shortener, rate limiter, social feed, notification system, ride-sharing, distributed cache. Time yourself (45 minutes each). Record yourself and watch back.

**Week 7 — Behavioral preparation.** Write out your STAR stories for each common category: impact/ownership, collaboration/conflict, failure/learning, growth/promotion, hiring/leadership. You need 6-8 stories that can be adapted across questions.

**Week 8 — First mock interviews.** Start doing mock interviews with real humans: Pramp, interviewing.io, or friends. At least 3 full coding mocks and 2 system design mocks this week. Mock interviews reveal your actual weaknesses better than solo practice.

Metrics for Month 2 completion: can complete 70%+ of mediums independently, have designed 6+ systems, have done 5+ mock interviews, behavioral stories written and practiced.

## Month 3: Peaking (Weeks 9-12)

**Week 9 — Hard problems and edge cases.** Spend time on hard LeetCode problems in your target areas. Focus on tree/graph problems (often appear at senior level), advanced DP, and string manipulation. Don't solve every hard problem — solve the patterns.

**Week 10 — Company-specific prep.** For your top 3-5 target companies: read their engineering blog, review Glassdoor and Blind for recent interview questions, understand their system at a high level. Tailor your system design examples to resonate with their domain.

**Week 11 — High-frequency mocks.** Aim for 2-3 mock interviews per day if possible. Full mock sessions: 45 min coding, 1 hour system design, 30 min behavioral. After each: write down what went well and what to fix. This feedback loop is the highest-leverage activity of the entire 90 days.

**Week 12 — Polish and scheduling.** Reduce new prep. Focus on reviewing your strongest material. Schedule your real interviews in clusters — same company's rounds close together, multiple companies' on-sites in the same week. Being in "interview mode" for a week is more effective than spreading interviews over two months.

## Common Mistakes That Kill 90-Day Prep Plans

**LeetCode-only preparation.** System design matters equally for senior roles. Engineers who only practice DSA fail system design rounds.

**Passive learning.** Watching solution videos isn't practice. Solving problems — especially struggling before checking hints — is what builds the pattern recognition.

**Not timing yourself.** Interview problems must be solved in 20-35 minutes. Practice with a timer from week 1, not week 11.

**Skipping behavioral prep.** Behavioral interviews eliminate candidates who aren't technically weak. Engineers routinely underinvest here because it feels like "not real prep."

**Random company applications.** Apply in batches with an intended start date. "I'll take the first offer I get" leads to rushed decisions or wasted applications on companies you wouldn't want to work at.

## Calibrating Effort to Your Target

The 90-day plan is calibrated for FAANG/top-tier companies. For smaller companies or mid-tier tech, Month 3's intensity is less critical — many companies use lighter interviews. For staff-level roles, add 2-3 additional weeks on organizational design and technical strategy problems.

The most important variable isn't hours per day — it's consistency. Thirty focused minutes every day for 90 days produces better results than 6-hour cramming sessions the week before interviews.
