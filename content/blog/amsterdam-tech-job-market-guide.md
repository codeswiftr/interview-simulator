The Netherlands punches well above its weight in tech. For a country of 18 million people, it hosts some of the most consequential engineering organizations in the world — and Amsterdam remains one of the most international tech cities in Europe. If you are considering a move, here is what you actually need to know.

## Why the Netherlands

Amsterdam was ranked among the top four most international tech hubs globally before the pandemic, and that character has stuck. English is the working language at every tech company of any size — this is not an exception or a perk, it is the baseline. You will not need Dutch to get hired or to do the job. You may want it eventually for daily life, but it is not a blocker.

The broader appeal: flat organizational hierarchies, short commutes, cycling infrastructure that actually works, universal healthcare, and a culture that values directness. The Dutch concept of work-life balance is real — long hours are not a badge of honor here. For engineers with families, international schools are widely available in the Randstad region.

## Who Is Hiring

### Enterprise and Global Scale

**Booking.com** is the largest private tech employer in Amsterdam. With over 5,000 engineers on-site, it operates one of the largest engineering organizations in Europe. Roles span backend, data, ML, infrastructure, and product engineering. The scale means deep specialization is possible.

**ASML** is arguably the most strategically important technology company in the world. Based in Eindhoven (90 minutes from Amsterdam, commutable or relocatable), ASML manufactures the only EUV lithography machines used to fabricate advanced semiconductors — every major chip foundry depends on them. Engineering roles here are genuinely hard-to-find globally: software, embedded systems, optics, mechatronics. ASML recruits internationally and aggressively.

**TomTom** (navigation and mapping technology) and **NXP Semiconductors** (automotive and embedded chips) are both Amsterdam-area employers with substantial engineering headcount. **Philips** (medtech and health) is headquartered in Eindhoven alongside ASML and runs significant R&D teams.

### Scale-Ups with Strong Engineering Reputations

**Adyen** (payment technology, publicly traded) is consistently cited by engineers as having one of the best engineering cultures in Europe. They process a meaningful share of global card payments and are selective in hiring. Compensation is at the top of the Dutch market.

**Miro** (collaborative whiteboard) runs a significant R&D hub in Amsterdam. **Elastic** (search and observability) has a strong Amsterdam presence. **WeTransfer**, **Catawiki**, **Takeaway.com / Just Eat**, and **MessageBird (now Bird)** are all Amsterdam-grown companies with engineering teams of varying sizes.

### Gaming

If games are your domain: **Guerrilla Games** (the Horizon Zero Dawn/Forbidden West series, a first-party PlayStation studio) is based in Amsterdam. **Niantic** (Pokemon Go) runs its European hub from Amsterdam. These are niche but notable if the industry matters to you.

## Salary Expectations

Netherlands salaries are mid-range for Western Europe — meaningfully higher than Berlin or Eastern European markets, lower than London or Zurich, roughly comparable to Paris or Stockholm.

| Level | Gross Annual |
|---|---|
| Junior (0–2 years) | €45,000–€65,000 |
| Mid-level (3–5 years) | €65,000–€90,000 |
| Senior (5–8 years) | €90,000–€130,000 |
| Staff / Principal | €130,000–€180,000 |

Adyen and ASML pay at the upper end for their respective roles. Early-stage startups vary widely and often compensate partly in equity. Amsterdam cost of living is expensive relative to the Netherlands as a whole — housing in particular is tight and pricey — but significantly cheaper than London for equivalent purchasing power.

## The 30% Tax Ruling — Read This Carefully

This is the most important financial benefit that engineers relocating to the Netherlands frequently overlook. The Dutch government offers the **30% ruling (30%-regeling)** for highly skilled migrants: 30% of your gross salary is treated as a tax-free cost reimbursement, effectively reducing your income tax burden substantially for up to five years.

The practical effect is significant. An engineer earning €90,000 gross can end up with a take-home comparable to what they would net from a higher nominal salary without the ruling. The requirements:

- You must be recruited from abroad (not already living in the Netherlands)
- Your salary must exceed a threshold (approximately €46,000 gross for most tech roles in 2024/2025)
- You must not have lived within 150km of the Dutch border in the prior 16 months

The ruling is applied jointly with your employer — they file the request, and approval typically comes within a few weeks of starting. If you are interviewing at Dutch companies, ask explicitly whether they support 30% ruling applications. Most established tech employers do. Some early-stage startups do not have the administrative infrastructure and will tell you upfront.

## Getting a Visa

For non-EU engineers, the standard path is the **Highly Skilled Migrant visa** (Kennismigrant). The process is employer-sponsored:

- Your employer must be a recognized IND sponsor (most established tech companies are)
- Salary thresholds apply: approximately €4,000/month gross for candidates under 30, approximately €5,500/month for candidates 30 and older (thresholds adjust annually)
- Processing time: typically 2–4 weeks once the employer submits
- The **EU Blue Card** is also available and may suit some candidates depending on qualification level

The Kennismigrant visa is employer-tied, so changing jobs requires a new application — but the process is straightforward for in-demand engineering roles.

## Interview Culture

Dutch directness shapes how interviews run. Expect honest feedback — good and bad — delivered without the social padding common in US interview culture. Hierarchy is flat; you may interview with your potential peers as often as with managers, and their opinion carries weight.

The technical bar is uneven across the market. ASML and Adyen run rigorous processes — system design, strong domain depth, multiple rounds. Most Amsterdam scale-ups use a take-home project plus a technical discussion rather than whiteboard algorithm exercises. Booking.com has historically leaned more toward classic engineering interview formats.

## Finding Roles

Direct applications to career pages at Booking.com, Adyen, ASML, and Guerrilla Games are worth doing. For broader search:

- **LinkedIn** — high signal for Amsterdam, most companies post here
- **Honeypot** — a tech-specific job platform with strong Netherlands coverage; engineers create profiles and companies approach them
- **TechNL** — community job board for the Dutch tech ecosystem
- **Relocate.me** — useful if you are still in the research phase and want to compare relocation packages

The Amsterdam tech community is connected enough that referrals move quickly. If you know anyone at a company you are targeting, use it.

---

The Netherlands is a serious option for engineers who want a high quality of life, an international environment, and access to technically challenging work — without relocating to London or Silicon Valley. The 30% ruling, in particular, changes the financial calculus in ways that are not obvious until you run the numbers. Do that before you negotiate your offer.
