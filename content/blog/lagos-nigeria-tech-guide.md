# Lagos Tech Scene Guide: Software Engineering Opportunities in Nigeria

Lagos is Africa's largest tech hub by headcount, funding, and ambition. With a population of over 20 million and a median age under 20, Nigeria's commercial capital is generating some of the continent's most innovative technology companies — and an increasing number of software engineers who work for global companies while based here. This guide covers everything you need to navigate Lagos's tech ecosystem as a job seeker or interview candidate.

## The Ecosystem

Lagos's tech scene has exploded since the mid-2010s. The Yaba neighborhood — nicknamed "Yabacon Valley" — has historically concentrated many startups, though the ecosystem has spread across Victoria Island, Lekki, and other areas.

**African-born fintech giants:** Paystack (acquired by Stripe), Flutterwave, PiggyVest, and Cowrywise were founded in Lagos. These companies handle billions of dollars in transactions and have engineering teams working on payment infrastructure that rivals anything in the world for complexity — reconciling multiple mobile money providers, bank APIs, and regulatory requirements across dozens of African countries.

**Logistics and mobility:** Gokada (now pivoted to logistics tech), MAX.ng, and Kobo360 are building last-mile delivery and trucking technology for West Africa's fragmented logistics market. These are genuinely hard engineering problems.

**Health tech and ed tech:** LifeBank (medical supply chain), Reliance Health, and Andela (which trains and places developers globally) represent the broader social impact tech sector.

**Global remote work:** A significant and growing segment of Lagos-based engineers work remotely for US, UK, or EU companies. Companies like Andela, Toptal, and various direct remote employers have created a generation of Lagos engineers earning international salaries.

## Salary Landscape

Nigerian tech salaries span a wide range depending on the employer type:

**Local startups and Nigerian companies:**
- Junior: 150,000–300,000 NGN/month
- Mid-level: 300,000–600,000 NGN/month
- Senior: 600,000–1,200,000 NGN/month

**USD-paying remote roles (increasingly common for senior engineers):**
- Mid-level: $2,500–$5,000/month
- Senior: $5,000–$10,000+/month

The difference between local and USD-denominated salaries is significant — USD-paying remote roles have become highly sought after as the naira has experienced substantial devaluation. Engineers with strong English communication skills and competitive portfolios increasingly target international remote roles.

## Interview Culture

Lagos tech interviews vary significantly by company type.

### At Nigerian Startups and Scale-Ups

**Practical assessments dominate.** Most Lagos fintech companies skip LeetCode-style puzzles entirely and focus on real-world engineering challenges. Paystack-alumni-founded companies tend to have thoughtful hiring processes that include system design and code review exercises.

**Communication is heavily evaluated.** Because many teams work across multiple African countries and with international partners, written and verbal communication clarity is often explicitly assessed. The ability to explain technical decisions to non-technical stakeholders matters.

**Portfolio and GitHub presence matter.** Open source contributions, personal projects, and visible technical output carry significant weight in Lagos hiring decisions, especially for engineers without brand-name employers on their resume.

### At Companies Hiring for Remote Roles

Expect international-standard technical interviews: algorithm problems, system design, behavioral rounds. Companies hiring Lagos-based engineers for USD-denominated remote roles typically run the same interview process as for engineers anywhere else in the world. This means LeetCode preparation is genuinely valuable.

**Andela's assessment process** (for engineers seeking global placement) is rigorous and structured — coding assessments, technical interviews, and soft skills evaluation. Many Lagos engineers use Andela as a launchpad for international remote careers.

## Technical Skills in Demand

- **JavaScript/TypeScript and Node.js** — dominant for backend in the startup ecosystem
- **Python** — growing fast in data engineering, ML, and fintech backend
- **React** — the default frontend framework
- **Flutter/Dart** — mobile development for African consumer apps
- **PostgreSQL and MongoDB** — the standard database choices at startups
- **Payment API integration** — Paystack, Flutterwave, and African mobile money APIs are highly specialized skills

## Preparing for a Lagos Tech Interview

**Understand the African payments landscape.** If you're interviewing at a fintech company, know how USSD works, what mobile money means in West Africa, and why reconciliation is hard across multiple providers. This contextual knowledge signals genuine engagement.

**Practice explaining your architectural decisions.** Lagos tech interviews, especially at mature startups, focus heavily on the "why" behind technical choices. Be prepared to defend your design decisions and discuss tradeoffs explicitly.

**Network through the community.** Lagos Tech Hub (LTH), Google Developer Groups, and the DevFest Nigeria conference are active networking venues. Many Lagos tech jobs are filled through community referrals.

**Consider the remote work path.** If your goal is a USD-denominated role, invest in building a strong GitHub profile, contributing to open source projects, and practicing international-standard technical interviews. Interview Simulator can help you prepare for the algorithmic and system design components of these processes.

## The Bigger Picture

Lagos engineers are increasingly recognized globally. The density of talent being produced by Nigerian universities, bootcamps like Decagon and HNG, and self-directed learning is remarkable. The combination of strong English, a large domestic market to learn on, and proximity to complex, frontier engineering problems — payments across fragmented markets, healthcare logistics without reliable infrastructure — is producing engineers with unusually broad problem-solving experience.

Whether you're a Lagos-based engineer targeting your first international role or a global engineer considering opportunities in Nigeria, understanding this market's unique dynamics is your competitive edge.
